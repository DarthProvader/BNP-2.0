**Apple**, via **Reuters** and **The Verge**, is training a China-focused language model with **Alibaba**’s help. **Google** will let users turn off the *visible* watermark on Gemini and Flow generations — while invisible **SynthID** and **C2PA** metadata stay on. **Z.ai** ships **GLM-5.3**, which tops an independent coding bench and helped surface **2,436** open-source vulnerabilities in the wild. **Ars Technica** charts a US–China price war in the mid-tier. And **YouTube** mistakenly treats **Kurzgesagt** like AI slop.

## The partner Washington did not schedule

For years **Apple** leaned on domestic Chinese models because US systems such as ChatGPT are unavailable there. Now, according to three people familiar with the matter cited by **Reuters**, it is building a *proprietary* China model with Alibaba’s support. **The Verge** reads it as a strategic break: more control over the product in the hardest regulatory theater, with Apple Intelligence expected “in the coming months” after an iOS update.

Apple already registered the on-device generative service with China’s cyberspace regulator. If clearance holds, Reuters says it would be the first US company approved to offer its own model on the Chinese market. That is not a footnote while Washington and Beijing treat AI as strategic feedstock. The Cupertino brand is learning a Hangzhou dialect — not from idealism, but because otherwise the phone on the shelf stays mute.

## Sparkle off, detector still cocked

**Josh Woodward** of Google said on X that Gemini and Flow will offer a *Media watermark* toggle — the corner sparkle — for Nano Banana, Omni, and Lyria, with Search soon. **TechCrunch** and **The Verge** stress the same bargain: visible marks optional, invisible **SynthID** and **C2PA** mandatory. Google is also open-sourcing **Credentio** so apps can validate locally.

The contrast with **Anthropic**’s recent watermark push is deliberate. Claude’s invisible marks split the user base. Google hides the sparkle so creative workflows look “clean,” and moves provenance into metadata most viewers never see. Transparency migrates from the pixel to a side channel — and anyone without a reader stays blind.

The same day, **Dexerto** and others report that YouTube’s AI-slop detector choked **Kurzgesagt**. The 25-million-subscriber science channel watched a microscopic-superpredators video post its worst reach since 2013 — even as click-through and watch time ran above average. YouTube confirmed the false positive. The system built to starve automated junk mistook carefully human animation for waste. One Google hand turns watermarks off. The other throttles a channel that warned about AI slop before the word became merch.

## An open model that hunts holes

**Z.ai** says **GLM-5.3** keeps the GLM-5.2 base — every gain is post-training. Coding jumps, long-horizon agency jumps, and *emergent* cyber capability grew faster than the lab expected. On CyberGym it scores 84.5%, ahead of Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. **AI Code King** put it at **73/80** on KingBench 3 — first place over Fable 5. Weights land in two weeks after safety hardening; **Open-Source Shield** rides alongside.

The sharper number lives on **r/singularity**: after expert review and deduplication, the model helped find **2,436** vulnerabilities across 269 projects, including **1,097** medium-to-high severity issues. Average age of the bugs: about 26 years. The ledger at **cvd.z.ai** tracks dozens of public disclosures and thousands still under embargo. This is not a marketing screenshot. It is open weights behaving like a patient audit team — and a reminder that the Chinese open stack is no longer competing only on token price.

## US labs discount the middle

**Ars Technica** (via the FT) maps the price war: **OpenAI** cut **GPT-5.6 Luna** by 80%, **Anthropic** priced **Opus 5** at half of Fable 5 and shelved a Sonnet 5 hike. Silicon Data says token prices from top US labs fell nearly a quarter since mid-July. The motive is blunt — companies such as DoorDash and Airbnb reach for cheaper Chinese models once usage-based billing starts to hurt.

Mantas Lukauskas of Hostinger put it cleanly: *US labs have cut the middle and are defending the top.* Flagships stay expensive. The mid-tier fights for customers who already know how to switch providers. Yesterday’s Ultrafast Sol sold latency. Today’s story sells a discount — and admits that frontier without a price list is not enough.

## Executive exits as an IPO footnote

**CNBC** folds yesterday’s departures of **Denise Dresser** and **Brad Lightcap** into one line: a *huge red flag* ahead of the IPO. OpenAI has a confidential filing, a valuation near **$852 billion**, and investors that **Sarah Friar** and **Greg Brockman** are meant to calm. Some insiders call the chaos “part of the culture.” Others read a roughly **$7 billion** secondary as unlocked golden handcuffs — once you can sell, you can leave.

This is not another Ultrafast launch note. It is a ledger entry for a company that accelerates inference in public while rotating leadership faster than a roadshow can land. On **r/technology** and **r/singularity**, the headline travels without mercy: talent exodus before listing.

## A conjecture proved by a doctor and Sol

**Jin Shanmu**, a neurosurgery resident and postdoc at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, was not hunting math glory. He was chasing a tool for transcranial ultrasound research. Along the way, with **GPT-5.6 Sol** on ChatGPT Work, **SCMP** and **r/singularity** report that he reached a proof of **Crouzeix’s conjecture** from 2004 — a numerical linear algebra problem where the best prior bound sat near 2.414 instead of the sharp constant 2.

The autonomous run lasted about **16 hours**. Experts, including Michel Crouzeix, examined the resulting preprint. This is not “AI replaced mathematicians” in a clean slogan. It is a doctor without a formal algebra PhD who knew how to steer a model — and a model that kept searching until an adversarial inner loop let a proof out. The same day **Matt Shumer** shows **Grok 4.6** surviving a **48-hour** Gauntlet Loop to ship a shooter. Long autonomous runs are stopping being demos. They are becoming a work method.

## In brief

**Andrew Ng** published *The AI Engineering Skills Map* — a chart of skills for building software the way 2026 actually works, not the way 2022 did. **SK Hynix** warns that **2027** will be the worst year in history for memory supply as AI infrastructure eats DRAM faster than fabs can print it. **TechCrunch** cautions hyperscalers betting on natural gas if regional US prices really triple. And on **r/ChatGPT**, Gemini sometimes recommends the competition — a small comic footnote on a day Google otherwise spent tuning watermarks and detectors.
