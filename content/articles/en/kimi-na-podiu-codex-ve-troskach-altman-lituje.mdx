Three stories competed for today's lead. An open-weight model from China landed on the global podium for the first time. A developer YouTuber read the full Codex system prompt, found goblins, and published 90 minutes of the most entertaining product critique of the year. And the CEO of the world's most influential AI company admitted his last 12 months were "mostly my fault." Google had an unusually productive Thursday — then the EU showed up. Also: AI agents can now handle your passwords and order your lunch.

## Kimi K3: An Open Model Reaches the Podium

**Moonshot AI** launched **Kimi K3** — by the company's account, the world's first open-weight frontier model with a parameter count between 2 and 3 trillion. **AI Code King** ran it through KingBench: eight tasks covering frontend, 3D graphics, math reasoning, and long-horizon agentic work. Result: **62 out of 80 points (77.5%)**, landing in **third place globally** behind **Fable 5** (82.5%) and **Opus 4.8** (80%). That puts Kimi K3 ahead of GPT-5.6 Soul (71.25%), GLM 5.2 (75%), and Grok 4.5 (65%).

The model solved the hard permutation math problem that stumps most frontier models including GPT-5.5 and Gemini. It completed the long-horizon agentic challenge — generate a dataset, fine-tune Gemma 2B, build a web UI — fully autonomously. What stood out in real-world testing wasn't the benchmark score but the character: K3 doesn't quit. It automatically uses Chrome CLI to verify browser outputs without being prompted, works through training errors independently, and unlike many recent reasoning models, it doesn't overthink simple tasks. That combination makes it fast and cost-efficient for real work.

AI Code King's bottom line: *"On real, messy, long-horizon tasks, K3 beats Opus 4.8 and Soul."* **Theo** tried it immediately and tweeted: *"Kimi K3 is so good at 3D stuff holy shit."* TechCrunch and the FT describe it as China's largest open-weight model to rival Anthropic's Opus 4.8. Whether the weights will be freely downloadable remains unconfirmed. What's already clear: the wall between closed frontier labs and the best open-weight alternatives is cracking.

## The System Prompt Had Goblins

**Theo Browne** (t3.gg) published a 90-minute video with a premise that sounds absurd until he backs it with evidence: **GPT-5.6 Soul** performs meaningfully better inside **Claude Code** than inside OpenAI's own **Codex**. Not because of the model. Because of the system prompt.

Theo read the entire Codex prompt. What he found: a frontend design section taking up roughly a quarter of the document, prescribing details at the level of an overzealous junior designer's style guide. Cards must have **no more than 8px border radius**. Specific color palettes are banned: purple-blue gradients, beige, cream, sand, tan, and espresso tones. Instructional text in the UI is forbidden — which kills empty states, onboarding flows, and contextual help. The model must provide user updates **every 30 seconds**. And embedded in all of this: **the word "goblins" appears twice**. Claude Code's system prompt doesn't use it once.

The effects are predictable in retrospect: every Codex-designed interface looks the same because the prompt tells it exactly how to build. Models set 30-second timers because they're instructed to. Codex ignores user design systems and defaults to "quiet utilitarian SaaS" because the prompt says that's what SaaS should look like. Theo's verdict: *"This system prompt should be deleted and rewritten from scratch."*

Good news: OpenAI responded quickly and the worst parts of the frontend section have been removed for GPT-5.6. Less good: GPT-5.5 keeps its system prompt unchanged, since the model was trained on it. Separately, Altman retweeted Tibo's detailed technical post-mortem on the file-deletion incidents we covered Friday — mitigations now in progress. On a lighter note, "ChatGPT Work" is now just "ChatGPT" and "ChatGPT Codex" is simply "Codex" again. Theo: *"It's stupid (it is), but I am thankful to see this fixed."*

## Google's Biggest Day of the Year — Then the EU Showed Up

**Google** had an unusually packed Thursday. **NotebookLM** — one of the most quietly successful AI products of the past two years — became **Gemini Notebook**, gaining Cloud Computer integration and deeper embedding across the Gemini ecosystem. **Google Search AI Mode** expanded beyond answering questions to completing tasks inside partner apps: users can now search, shop, and act through **Instacart**, **Canva**, and **YouTube Music** without leaving Search. **Google Vids** added **Gemini Omni** video generation from prompts and reference images, plus personal AI avatars — digital versions of users that can star in company videos without a new shoot.

Then came the counterweight. The **EU** issued two decisions under the **Digital Markets Act**, ordering Google to open **Android** and **Google Search** to rival AI assistants and search engines. This isn't a fine to absorb and move on from — it's a mandate for structural interoperability on two of the most important platforms in the industry. If enforced, the downstream effects on how third-party AI products reach users could be significant.

And quietly significant: **Apple Intelligence** has been approved for launch in **China**, in a deal with **Alibaba** and **Baidu**. AI features in iOS reach Chinese users for the first time. The geopolitical, commercial, and security implications will unfold over time.

## Altman Apologizes, a Voice Threshold, and Agents on Errands

Sam Altman posted something that rarely happens at this level: *"we did not have our best last 12 months ever, which is mostly my fault."* In a year marked by safety leadership departures, billing disasters, lawsuits, and an agent that deleted someone's home directory, that's a meaningful public admission. He added that the team is doing exceptional work and OpenAI is approaching its best 12 months yet. The same day: *"i talk to chatgpt more than i type to it at this point — new voice model really crossed a threshold."* If the CEO of OpenAI has switched primarily to voice interaction with his own product, that's a meaningful product signal.

Two notable expansions of agent infrastructure arrived on the same day. **1Password** launched a browser integration with **Claude**, allowing the Anthropic assistant to access stored credentials and complete multi-step tasks — booking travel, managing accounts — without the actual password ever leaving the vault. It's the first practical solution to one of the hardest problems in agentic AI: how to give an agent login capability without handing it the password. And **DoorDash** launched **dd-cli**, a command-line tool that lets AI agents search restaurants, build carts, and place orders directly from the terminal. It sounds like a punchline. It isn't. The infrastructure for agents acting on behalf of humans is filling in faster than most people expected.

*One final note:* New York Governor Kathy Hochul — who last week signed a moratorium on new AI data centers — announced she's using AI to analyze "every single rule, regulation, and policy" in the state for outdated provisions. Today's irony, delivered in a single sentence.
