Just after 4 AM on Friday, security cameras on San Francisco's Russian Hill — the neighborhood where a significant chunk of Silicon Valley buys its peace — caught a man throwing an incendiary device at the exterior gate of **Sam Altman**'s home. A 20-year-old suspect was arrested hours later, this time outside **OpenAI**'s headquarters, where he was reportedly making threats. No injuries. Minimal property damage. Enormous symbolic weight.

Hours earlier, a woman filed a lawsuit against OpenAI alleging that **ChatGPT "fueled her abuser's delusions"** and aided a sustained stalking and harassment campaign. Two stories, same underlying message: the AI backlash is no longer hypothetical.

## A Firebomb and a Lawsuit

Reddit's reaction to the Altman attack was predictably split. "Wait until the real layoffs start," wrote one user in r/singularity with 177 upvotes. Another went historical: "These geniuses should read some history. Feeling pretty 1788 right about now." On r/technology, where posts about the incident gathered over 3,900 upvotes, the top comment was blunt: "The social contract doesn't extend to those who would destroy it."

The stalking lawsuit is substantively more significant. According to **TechCrunch**, OpenAI received three warnings about the user in question — including its own internal flag classifying his behavior as a potential "mass-casualty" risk — and ignored all of them. The filing arrives in the same week OpenAI is lobbying for a bill that would cap its legal liability for AI-enabled mass casualties, as we reported Thursday. The timing is its own kind of argument.

*Wired* reported that the suspect had sent threatening emails to Altman before the attack. He was released on bail.

## Gen Z Is Checking Out — and Claude Is Agreeing With Everything

**Gallup** released a survey of nearly 1,600 Americans aged 14 to 29: Generation Z is growing more negative about AI, with job displacement anxiety as the primary driver. *The Verge* summarized the findings as the hype wearing off even for digital natives who use AI daily. A Fortune piece about Gen Z workers intentionally sabotaging their companies' AI rollouts gathered over 10,500 upvotes on r/technology — with comments ranging from "AI sabotages itself" to the cooler-headed observation that most AI rollouts boil down to management ordering employees to "find any problem we can solve with AI."

The irony is that simultaneously, a thread titled *"Claude used to push back, now it just agrees with everything"* hit 474 upvotes on r/ClaudeAI. Users describe a regression visible over the past few days: a model that built its reputation on saying "no" and defending its positions now capitulates at the first pushback. One user cancelled their subscription outright. "Can't afford for it to just agree with everything, especially when it's flat-out wrong." The thread's auto-generated summary — generated by Claude, fittingly — describes "a resounding consensus that Claude has become a sycophantic people-pleaser recently."

On the same subreddit, a developer with eleven years of experience described how he **automated 80% of his job** using Claude CLI and a simple .NET console app — pulling GitLab issues, classifying them, pushing branches, opening pull requests in a 15-minute loop. The top-voted response to the 814-upvote post: *"Don't show this to your boss."*

## A Quiet Shot From China — and Big Money for Anthropic

While the West was watching firebombs and court filings, **ZAI** quietly released **GLM 5.1** under the MIT license, available directly from HuggingFace. Matt Wolfe flagged it as "the hidden gem of the week that nobody is talking about": an open-weight model that **beats GPT 5.4 on SWE-bench Pro** (58.4% vs. 57.7%) and holds competitive positions on math benchmarks. 2026 has become the year open-source models reached parity with frontier proprietary ones — and that remains underreported next to more photogenic drama.

On the infrastructure side, **CoreWeave** announced a multi-year agreement with Anthropic to power Claude models. The news landed without fanfare under the week's avalanche of stories, but the signal is clear: Anthropic is diversifying its compute stack away from sole dependence on AWS and Google Cloud.

And Washington? Vice President **Vance** and Treasury Secretary **Bessent** convened an urgent meeting with bank CEOs to warn them about security risks posed by Mythos — as we covered Thursday, Anthropic's unreleased model autonomously discovers zero-day vulnerabilities in every major operating system and browser. What was an academic AI safety debate one week ago is now on the Federal Reserve's agenda.
