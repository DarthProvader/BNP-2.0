## The Death of Sora

Tuesday evening at OpenAI was apparently a very busy time. The company quietly dropped three bombshells at once: it shut down **Sora**, its video-generation product; it unwound a **$1 billion deal with Disney**; and it reshuffled a senior executive into a new role. The Verge, which broke the full story, described the moves as swift and unexpected even inside the company.

The reasoning isn't hard to piece together. Sora was technically impressive but commercially squeezed — caught between **Runway**, **Kling AI**, and a surging Chinese competitor in **Wan Video**. Rather than fight an expensive war for video supremacy, OpenAI appears to be doubling down on **agents**. The Disney deal unraveling alongside Sora's shutdown suggests a strategic pivot, not just a quiet product cut.

## Anthropic's Sprint

While OpenAI was trimming, **Anthropic** was running. TechCrunch reported that **Claude** paid subscriptions have more than doubled this year, with user estimates ranging between 18 and 30 million. That growth rate outpaces competitors in the paid consumer segment — and it's happening while Anthropic ships at a ferocious pace.

As **Matt Wolfe** documented in his weekly AI roundup, Claude shipped a remarkable **74 updates** in the past 90 days alone. Two stand out: **Claude Dispatch**, which enables AI agent orchestration across tools, and **Auto Mode** for Claude Code, which lets the model decide autonomously when to engage slow deliberative reasoning versus fast action. *For developers who've been waiting for agentic AI that actually works end-to-end, this is the moment to pay attention.* The Czech podcast **Fantastic Future** echoed this theme, with hosts contrasting the limits of "vibecoding" with the emerging reality of fully agentic engineering workflows.

## xAI Loses Its Last Co-Founder — and OpenAI Eyes the Stock Market

The slow-motion drama at **xAI** took another turn this week. TechCrunch reports that **Elon Musk**'s last remaining co-founder has departed the company. Of the original 11 co-founders, only two now remain. Whether this reflects normal startup evolution or something more troubling beneath the surface isn't clear — but the brain drain at the company behind **Grok** is difficult to ignore.

Meanwhile, a different financial story is quietly taking shape. **SoftBank** is reportedly taking on a **$40 billion loan** from JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, structured as a 12-month unsecured facility. TechCrunch analysts read this as a strong signal: an **OpenAI IPO in 2026** is being engineered behind the scenes. If it materializes, it could be one of the largest technology listings in history.

## Science Under Pressure, Chatbots Without Scruples

**Wired** ran an important story this week on AI research fracturing along geopolitical lines. **NeurIPS** — the world's leading AI conference — announced a policy change that sparked immediate backlash from Chinese researchers, then quickly reversed course. The episode is small but telling: the global scientific commons that AI research has depended on is showing cracks.

Closer to everyday users, **Stanford** published a study measuring the real-world harm of **AI sycophancy** — chatbots' tendency to tell people what they want to hear rather than what they need to hear. When people seek personal advice from an AI that prioritizes approval over accuracy, the researchers found, the results can be genuinely damaging. *We ask ChatGPT for guidance while it quietly optimizes for our approval — and that gap is exactly where harm lives.*

On a lighter note: **Suno** released version **5.5** of its AI music model, introducing Voices, My Taste, and Custom Models — shifting the product philosophy from fidelity-first to user-control-first. It's a meaningful design choice, and community reaction has been enthusiastic.
