Just before midnight Pacific time, Anthropic posted a terse update on X: the **Department of Commerce** had lifted export controls on **Claude Fable 5** and **Claude Mythos 5**, and the company would begin restoring global access on July 1 — today. The developer community's reaction was immediate and jubilant. Matt Shumer wrote that one tweet was about to dramatically change his productivity for the next month. Matt Wolfe wondered aloud whether his current plan tier would still cover access. Theo from t3.gg rushed out a late-night explainer video to address what he called widespread misunderstandings in the community.

The political irony is difficult to ignore. The same Trump administration that ordered Anthropic to suspend access for foreign nationals — on the grounds that the models were too capable — reversed that decision within six weeks. The precise dynamics of what happened in Washington remain opaque; Wired quotes only "a senior White House official." Whatever the negotiation looked like behind closed doors, the outcome is clear: **Fable 5** is back. And Matt Shumer, who spent the downtime building for **Alpha School**, gave an immediate preview of what Fable can do that other models can't — a Pixar-quality educational video from a single prompt, an entire Hogwarts castle rendered in 3D, a self-generating game that fuses what a child needs to learn with what they're actually into. "We're going to transform education for one billion kids," he wrote. The demonstration made the weeks of waiting feel visceral.

## Sonnet 5 and Claude Science: Two More Punches on the Same Day

It would have been a perfectly eventful Monday even if Anthropic had stopped at the Fable news. Instead, the company shipped two more things in the same 24-hour window.

**Claude Sonnet 5** is Anthropic's new default model for agentic workloads — cheaper than Opus, faster, and surprisingly capable. Developer Alex Finn summarized it on X as only the community can: *"Dominates Opus 4.6. Almost as good as Opus 4.8. A fraction of the price."* According to TechCrunch, Anthropic is positioning Sonnet 5 as a direct, affordable alternative to Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, and Gemini Pro for applications that run hundreds or thousands of agent calls at scale. Theo warned his audience: Sonnet 5 is *not* a downgrade, it's a different category entirely. For production workloads where every API call has a cost, the economics shift significantly.

Running in parallel — quite literally — **Claude Science** was unveiled at a private summit for pharmaceutical executives and biotech founders in San Francisco. MIT Technology Review describes it as the scientific research analog of Claude Code: give it a high-level instruction and it will autonomously navigate databases, run computational pipelines, and iterate through results. TechCrunch's framing is precise: *"Anthropic is betting on workflow, not a new model, to win over scientists."* Researchers today juggle dozens of specialized tools. Claude Science is the bid to become their unified workspace.

## Chips, Robots, and Loops: The Rest of the World Kept Moving

While Anthropic commanded the headlines, several significant stories ran the risk of being overlooked.

**Etched**, the startup building specialized AI inference chips, emerged from stealth with staggering numbers: a **$5 billion valuation**, **$800 million raised**, and **$1 billion in committed customer contracts** before a single rack has been delivered — shipments start this summer. Andrej Karpathy retweeted the announcement without comment. Etched's core claim is that a chip designed exclusively for inference can outperform GPUs on throughput, latency, and power efficiency. If that holds in production, it is a direct challenge to **Nvidia**'s most profitable market segment.

From the robotics world, **NVIDIA GEAR** lab published remarkable research. Jim Fan and a team spanning NVIDIA, UMich, Berkeley, and CMU introduced **ASPIRE** — the first automated skill discovery system for robots. Rather than solving each task from scratch, ASPIRE continuously builds and refines a library of reusable skills that transfer from simulation to real hardware, and across different robot embodiments. Fan's formulation is elegant: *"a robot solving its 100th task is no longer as clueless as solving its first."* This generalization capability is precisely the barrier that has frustrated robotics for decades.

**Andrew Ng** meanwhile put a name to something developers have been doing intuitively for months: *"loop engineering."* The term spread across X after mentions by Boris Cherny, creator of Claude Code, and Peter Steinberger, creator of **OpenClaw**. Ng describes how closed agentic loops — where an AI agent writes code, tests it, and iterates without human intervention — have fundamentally changed how software gets built. OpenClaw underscored the point by shipping its **Android and iOS** apps on the same day. Agentic coding is now pocket-sized.

One note for readers following the European AI landscape: **EquiLibre Technologies**, a Prague-based AI lab founded by three ex-DeepMind researchers — the team behind a poker-playing AI — has been valued at over **$500 million** and is now building tools for quantitative hedge funds. Prague's presence on the global AI research map is not accidental. It's the compounding result of years of serious work, and EquiLibre is one more proof of that.
