Today's AI news could be filed under one theme: the gap between what powerful organizations say and what they do. **Sam Altman** says AI creates jobs — while his company quietly buried its most beloved developer product. **Meta** enters the frontier race with mixed results. **Elon Musk** declares his own product the most neutral AI on the planet. *Checks out.*

## The CEO Who Didn't Expect This

Yesterday, Sam Altman posted what may be the most self-aware thing an AI CEO has said in months: *"so far at least, i'm pretty sure AI has been net job-creating. this was not what i expected — although i was much less pessimistic than others, i thought by this level of capability we'd have seen some impact. it is possible this direction keeps going!"*

The phrasing matters. He's not announcing a finding — he's sharing a surprised intuition. The question this blog is named after — are they taking our jobs? — still doesn't have a clean answer. Economists disagree; industry splits along who's profiting from AI and who isn't. But when the CEO of the most influential AI lab says "I didn't expect this" instead of crowing victory, it carries a different weight than a press release would.

On the same day, OpenAI posted a job listing for a dedicated product manager focused on families, caregivers, and older adults — a signal the company is moving beyond tech-savvy early adopters. An internal analyst shared that GPT-5.6 Sol's growth in a single day exceeded the previous two weeks combined; Altman replied with one word: *"whoa."* The mainstream moment has arrived.

## The Brand That Became a Toggle

Theo Browne's video "The unexpected death of Codex" is worth watching even if you've never used the app. It's a precise autopsy of how you kill a beloved product while making the sensible business decision.

OpenAI folded the Codex app into ChatGPT. Codex is now a mode — a toggle, a tab inside a different product. The original app icon can still be chosen in settings, which Theo immediately did, adding: *"this is the only remaining proof of Codex's existence."* The strategic logic is coherent: the agentic capabilities Codex pioneered are too valuable to keep sequestered from a billion ChatGPT users. The Codex brand had also suffered from chronic identity confusion — model, CLI, specialized model variant, desktop app — and the merger could have resolved that cleanly.

But execution crushed what was working. Jensen Huang had emailed Sam Altman personally about his excitement over Codex. People wore Codex merch to events. Theo framed the loss precisely: *"instead of 'hey man, you gotta try Codex,' it's now 'hey man, you gotta try the Codex mode in the new ChatGPT desktop app.'"* Codex was too good for Codex — that's the epitaph Theo proposed, and it lands.

Theo's response was practical: he already had **T3 Code**, an open-source alternative he built "just in case." A quarter of T3 Code users run their own fork or patched version — that's what open-source is for. The community also found a workaround: using **CLIProxyAPI**, developers can now run GPT-5.6-Sol inside Claude Code with a simple alias. The harness and the model don't have to come from the same company.

## Meta's First Frontier Report Card

As we covered yesterday, Meta entered the frontier model race with **Muse Spark 1.1**. Today, **AI Code King** ran it through KingBench — seven tasks, ten points each. Result: **48/70 (68.57%), sixth place** on the leaderboard. Above GPT-5.6 Terra, Opus 4.7, and Sonnet 5; below Grok 4.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, GLM 5.2, Opus 4.8; and Fable 5 remains at the top.

The performance profile is more interesting than the rank. Muse Spark's UI output is genuinely beautiful — even on tasks where the underlying logic broke, the visual results looked polished. It nailed a permutation counting problem with the correct answer of 2460, *"a question that destroys most models"* including GPT-5.5, Opus 4.7, and Gemini 3.5 Flash. The fully autonomous task — generate a dataset, fine-tune Gemma 2B locally, build a working web UI — ran end-to-end without intervention. Full marks.

The failure modes are equally specific: in parallel multi-agent setups, the model cheerfully overwrites other agents' files without checking what exists. Complex simulation logic broke. Consistency is unpredictable. At $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens (input/output) — cheaper than Sonnet, with $20 free credits at API signup — the value proposition is real. For a first genuine frontier attempt from Meta, this is a respectable debut. The next version, if it fixes the file-awareness issues, could be a serious contender.

## "Grok: Most Neutral AI." Grok's Owner Declares.

Elon Musk retweeted multiple posts about a study by something called the *Neutrality Project*, which measured political neutrality across 18 AI models. Grok 4.5 scored **-0.02**; the average for others was **-0.41**, with "progressive bias" cited on environmental and social issues. Musk added: *"Grok is the most politically neutral and objectively truth-seeking AI."*

The skeptical notes are worth stating plainly. The Neutrality Project is a new organization with no documented track record or disclosed methodology. More fundamentally, "political neutrality" in AI is contested terrain: scientific consensus on climate change or vaccines isn't a left-wing position — it's established empirical knowledge. A model that treats that consensus as politically equivalent to its opposition will register differently on these scales, but that's a design choice masquerading as balance, not neutrality. xAI is clearly positioning Grok as the "fair and balanced" option for users skeptical of other labs — whether the model delivers on that framing will be answered in practice by people who aren't Elon Musk.

One aside: **Claude Code Desktop** received a sandboxed browser for accessing documentation and web resources directly from the IDE — a smaller feature with genuine workflow benefits for developers who live in the terminal.
