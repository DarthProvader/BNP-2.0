## The Architect Switched Sides

In 2017, **Noam Shazeer** co-authored a paper at Google Research with the modest title "Attention Is All You Need." It introduced the Transformer architecture that now underlies virtually every major AI system on the planet. Today, he announced he is leaving Google to join **OpenAI**.

**Sam Altman** responded on X without restraint: *"Noam is one of the people I have most wanted to work with since the very beginning of OpenAI. Only took 10 years. I think it will be worth the wait."* Shazeer co-invented the Transformer, left Google in 2021 to co-found **Character.AI**, and was effectively brought back into Google's orbit in 2024 when the company licensed his startup's technology and absorbed most of its team. Now, free of that arrangement, he's heading to OpenAI.

The timing is deliberate. OpenAI is building toward what it publicly describes as AGI, and Shazeer belongs to a very small group of people who understand the attention mechanism — the conceptual core of the architecture — from first principles rather than from documentation. The AI community's reaction ranged from stunned to superlative. *"One of the most significant talent moves in AI history."* For once, the hyperbole might not be hyperbole.

## An Impossible Condition

While Shazeer's move dominated the timeline, **Anthropic** is still fighting to bring **Fable 5** and **Mythos 5** back online. Today brought two new pieces of the puzzle, and neither is reassuring.

**Wired** revealed the hidden trigger for the blackout that's dominated AI news this week: the White House ordered Anthropic to revoke **SK Telecom**'s access to Claude Mythos over alleged ties to China — before the broader shutdown order that took the models offline for everyone. The US government appears to have concluded that SK Telecom was channeling advanced model capabilities to entities subject to export controls. Anthropic complied with the targeted revocation. Then came the wider order.

The more consequential revelation is the condition attached to bringing Fable 5 back: Trump administration officials told Wired that Anthropic must guarantee the model cannot be jailbroken. Security experts are unanimous: that's not achievable. No architecture makes that guarantee possible — no commitment survives sufficiently creative adversarial pressure. Wired's framing was precise: *"The White House is demanding a promise no one in the world can make."*

The geopolitical fallout is widening. At the **G7 summit**, French President **Emmanuel Macron** and Indian PM **Narendra Modi** formally raised the concern that the US could cut off allied nations from American AI overnight — a hypothetical the Anthropic blackout converted into a lived experience. Per TechCrunch, both governments have begun treating AI sovereignty as a security question, not a philosophical one. *The dependency on American AI infrastructure stopped being theoretical this week.*

**Matt Shumer** offered a window into what's actually at stake. While Fable remained offline, he shared a demo he built in two days for education platform **Alpha School**: a 3D game that regenerates itself in real-time based on what each child needs to learn and what they're genuinely interested in. Built with ThreeJS, with GPT-Realtime-2 handling the child's voice interactions. Shumer was blunt: *"Try doing this with Opus. It's nearly impossible. You could spend hundreds of hours and not get close."*

## From Cats to Full-Body Scans

The most unexpected product announcement of the week came from a company known for AI-generated fantasy art.

**Midjourney** unveiled its first-ever hardware product — not a robot, not a display, not an AI assistant. The **Midjourney Scanner** is a medical device: a full-body ultrasound system using a ring of sensors to capture vertical cross-sections of the entire body. CEO **David Holz** revealed it at a live event in San Francisco, acknowledging what every observer is already thinking: the company famous for "cat pictures" is now building medical-grade imaging hardware.

**Matt Wolfe**, one of AI's most-followed commentators, reacted simply: *"NOT what I expected AT ALL."* Midjourney plans to deploy the scanner in spa-like wellness settings — targeting the preventive health market, where routine screenings are repositioning themselves as consumer products rather than clinical events. *An AI image generator pivoting to ultrasound hardware. In 2022, that sentence wouldn't have made it into science fiction as a plausible premise.*

## The Trust Gap

**Pew Research** dropped a new survey today, and the numbers carry the blunt clarity that only population-scale data can provide. Just **16 percent** of Americans believe AI will have a positive impact on society. **63 percent** think the technology is advancing too quickly. Meanwhile, **49 percent** report using chatbots at least occasionally. Half the country uses AI while distrusting it. The Verge called it correctly: AI has become a technology that is omnipresent but not accepted.

This gap is illustrated sharply by the "tokenmaxxing" reckoning now playing out. The trend — Silicon Valley CEOs urging employees to push AI usage as far as it would go, regardless of cost — has met its ledger. **Uber** reportedly burned through its entire annual AI budget in a matter of months. **Meta** killed its internal AI usage leaderboard. Companies have started trimming Claude licenses. **Tiffany Luck** of NEA Capital, speaking to TechCrunch, described the shift underway: enterprises are moving from measuring token consumption to measuring ROI. *The gold rush phase is over. The unglamorous part begins.*

On the other end of the spectrum, results that require no asterisks. Google published a **Nature** study on **AMIE**, its conversational medical AI, showing it matches primary care physicians in managing complex chronic conditions — not in scheduling or paperwork, but in clinical decision-making. That's one of the cleaner data points of the week. And **Odyssey**, building *world models* — AI systems that model physical and causal relationships rather than just language patterns — secured a **$1.45 billion valuation** backed by Amazon and others, positioning itself as one of the names to watch as the industry looks past LLMs toward architectures that reason about how things actually work.
