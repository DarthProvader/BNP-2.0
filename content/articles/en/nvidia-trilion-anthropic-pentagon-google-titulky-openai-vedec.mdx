## Nvidia Says a Trillion. Wall Street Says: Show Me.

**NVIDIA**'s GTC conference wrapped up with CEO Jensen Huang delivering his signature leather-jacket keynote — two and a half hours of vision, hype, and hardware. The headline number: **$1 trillion** in AI chip sales projected by 2027, and a new imperative that every company needs an "OpenClaw strategy." A demo robot named Olaf had its microphone cut mid-performance — an accidental but fitting metaphor for a technological revolution that works, just not always as planned.

Wall Street, however, was not impressed. As **TechCrunch** reports, investors remain wary of an AI bubble even as the industry insists the boom is just beginning. Nvidia's confidence is real; whether it translates into sustained earnings or overinflated expectations remains the open question.

## Can Anthropic Sabotage AI During a War? The Pentagon Thinks So. Anthropic Disagrees.

The legal confrontation between **Anthropic** and the U.S. Department of Defense reached new levels of drama this week. New court filings, first reported by **TechCrunch**, reveal the Pentagon privately told Anthropic the two sides were "nearly aligned" — this, just one week after Trump publicly declared the relationship finished. Meanwhile, **Wired** uncovered the DoD's most explosive allegation yet: that Anthropic could theoretically *sabotage* its own AI tools in the middle of an active military conflict.

Anthropic executives flatly deny this is possible and have submitted two sworn declarations to a California federal court, arguing the government's case rests on fundamental technical misunderstandings. *Is this a legal technicality or a genuine national security question?* A federal judge will have to decide — but either way, it's a stark reminder of how unprepared our institutions are for AI in warfare.

## OpenAI's New Goal: Science Without Scientists

**MIT Technology Review** reports a significant strategic shift at **OpenAI**: the company is refocusing its entire research operation on building what it calls a fully automated "AI researcher." Not a smarter assistant. Not a better chatbot. A fully autonomous, agent-based system capable of independently formulating hypotheses, designing experiments, and tackling complex scientific problems — without human direction.

If OpenAI pulls this off, the implications extend far beyond productivity gains. The thing that makes science science — the curious, fallible, persistent human mind chasing an answer — would become optional. That's either the most exciting or most alarming sentence in today's newsletter, depending on where you sit.

## Trump Silences the States; Google Quietly Rewrites the Headlines

The Trump administration released its AI legislative blueprint this week, and its message was unmistakable: federal preemption of state AI laws, lighter-touch regulation in the name of innovation, and an explicit national goal of "global AI dominance." As **The Verge** and **TechCrunch** both note, individual states — which have functioned as regulatory laboratories — would effectively lose authority to act under this framework.

While policymakers battle over rules, **Google** is already changing the game on the ground. The Verge revealed that Google Search is quietly replacing real news headlines with AI-generated alternatives in some results. Journalists aren't just writing *about* AI — AI is now rewriting their work at the exact moment most readers encounter it.

## Hachette Says No to AI Fiction; DoorDash Says Yes to AI Labor

Publisher **Hachette Book Group** pulled the horror novel *Shy Girl* from publication after suspecting the text was AI-generated, per **TechCrunch** — a rare, public stance from a major publisher that has until now stayed quiet on the issue.

The story lands alongside a Wired investigation into **DoorDash's Tasks app**, where gig workers are paid to film themselves doing laundry, scrambling eggs, and taking walks in the park — all to generate training data for AI systems. This is the real shape of the AI labor economy: no contract, no security, and the work you do today trains the system that replaces you tomorrow.
