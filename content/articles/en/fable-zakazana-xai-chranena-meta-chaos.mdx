## Day Four: Negotiators in Washington, Defenders in Protest

As we've covered since Friday, **Anthropic** pulled **Fable 5** and **Mythos 5** offline after a US government export control directive. Monday brought new rounds — but no resolution.

Anthropic assembled a technical team and flew to Washington. **Logan Graham** (Frontier Red Team), **Dave Orr** (head of safeguards), and **Nicholas Carlini** (lead security researcher) gave an in-person presentation to ONCD and Commerce officials on Monday. Reporting Theo T3.gg shared from Sophia Cai detailed the weekend: Trump asked Commerce Secretary **Howard Lutnick** to take a leading role; Saturday calls ran over an hour. By Monday evening: still no agreement. Fable offline, day four.

Dozens of cybersecurity veterans signed an open protest letter to the White House. The argument is precise: the model the government blocked as a security threat is exactly the tool defenders need for code analysis, vulnerability discovery, and threat hunting. TechCrunch's **Zack Whittaker** cut through the noise: *"The ban was never about the jailbreak — the message is clear: The AI industry isn't immune from U.S. government interference."*

**Matt Shumer**, who just joined education startup **Alpha School** to build AI-powered learning tools for a billion kids, had been generating a Pixar-quality teaching video with a single Fable prompt. Access cut off mid-generation. *The video stopped in the middle of a sentence.*

## One Week, Two Standards

Wednesday brought the week's defining contrast. The **Department of Justice** stood in federal court defending **xAI** — Elon Musk's AI company — from an NAACP lawsuit over polluting gas turbines in Memphis. The government's argument, per Wired: xAI is *"vital to national security, including operations in the Iran War."*

The same week, Washington banned the models of an AI company built around safety — and defended the polluting operations of an AI company as essential military infrastructure. The difference isn't technical. It's political.

**The Verge** named the consequence plainly: *"Trump's Anthropic shutdown just made the case for non-American AI."* When the US government can pull the world's most capable public AI model overnight, every government outside the US faces the same question: can you build critical national infrastructure on American proprietary AI? The answer being drafted in Berlin, New Delhi, and Beijing is increasingly no.

At Stanford's graduation, **Sundar Pichai** faced boos and a student walkout over Google's defense contracts and ICE ties. Public sentiment on Big Tech and military partnerships is shifting faster than companies can respond.

## Meta's Triple Monday

While Anthropic sparred with the White House, three separate uncomfortable stories broke out of Menlo Park — each of which would normally anchor a full news day on its own.

**Andrew Bosworth**, Meta's CTO, sent an internal memo — obtained by Wired — admitting the company's AI reorganization had been *"atrocious."* He promised better communication, more stability, and the return of perks that were quietly eliminated. *You don't write that memo from organizational strength.* It follows June reporting describing Meta's AI unit as a "soul-crushing gulag" — except this time the language comes from the CTO himself.

The same day, Wired reported that Meta hired **Rank One Computing** — a firm whose board includes a former CIA deputy director and former FBI science chief — to prototype face recognition for **Ray-Ban Meta** smart glasses. Meta calls it internal experimentation, never deployed. *The choice of vendor makes its own statement.*

Rolling out to users today: **AI Mode** on Facebook, an AI search experience drawing from public posts across Meta's platforms. If you've written a public post on Facebook or Instagram, it now feeds an answer engine. Meta wants to catch OpenAI and Google. It's using the data it has.

## The Redistribution

TechCrunch's **Connie Loizos** published the day's most important quiet piece: the AI layoff wave is becoming a powder keg. Tens of thousands of workers are losing jobs while a small group of AI insiders accumulates wealth at nearly incomprehensible scale. The structural resentment — AI teams receiving unprecedented compensation while the rest collect severance — is becoming public anger.

**Salesforce** acquired AI customer service platform **Fin** for **$3.6 billion** — one of the year's largest AI acquisitions — to fold into its Agentforce enterprise platform. **NewCore** raised $66 million to solve a problem that barely existed two years ago: giving AI agents identities, credentials, and accountability when they operate as enterprise employees. An agent with access to a payment system needs something like HR. Nothing like that exists yet — and that's an increasingly fundable gap.

In Bangalore, **Sarvam** became India's newest AI unicorn with a **$234 million** funding round led by HCLTech. The global AI race doesn't wait for political disputes.
