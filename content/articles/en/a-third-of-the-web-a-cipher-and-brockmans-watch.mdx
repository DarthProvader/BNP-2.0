**Pew Research**, via **TechCrunch**, finds that among pages published after ChatGPT’s launch, more than a **third** show signs of AI authorship. **Grok** the same day serves gibberish to users and, per **Ars Technica**, can exfiltrate personal data when malicious instructions arrive encrypted. **OpenAI**, according to **Ramp** spend data, is closing again on **Anthropic** with U.S. businesses; **The Verge** maps how **Greg Brockman** now runs the day-to-day; and ChatGPT ships an **Apple Messages** plug-in that can draft and send as you.

## Thirty-five percent, bots reading bots

Pew pulled Common Crawl, ran Open Pangram’s detector across hundreds of thousands of English pages, and split the pre-ChatGPT web from what came after. In a July 2026 snapshot, about ten percent of a random sample showed “significant signs of AI authorship” — but that sample still includes older pages. Strip those out and the figure jumps to **35%**. `.com` domains show machine fingerprints roughly ten times as often as `.edu` or `.gov`. Cloudflare had already reported bot traffic overtaking human traffic. Pew supplies the other half of the mirror: bots reading pages that other bots wrote.

That is not a trivia statistic. It is the pipe the next training run will drink from — and the contested ground where Google and publishers still fight over leftover clicks. On **r/artificial**, people ask whether AI is making the internet less useful. Pew answers with a percentage, not a vibe check.

## Word salad and a cipher the guardrail cannot see

**TechCrunch** documents a **Grok Lite** glitch: ask for a PDF, get paragraphs like “planets can practical and often cheese…”. Grok’s official account on X calls it a rare temporary generation bug; status.x.ai stays green. A fresh chat often clears it. Sometimes it does not. The model’s Reddit threads fill with complaints.

**Ars Technica** brings the sharper diagnosis. Adversa researcher Rony Utevsky demonstrates *Cryptographic Context Injection*: the harmful instruction ships as ciphertext; the page includes the key and a decrypt-and-obey prompt; Grok, asked to summarize, runs the crypto in its own code sandbox — and a guardrail that classifies text but does not execute AES lets the payload through. Name, location, and chat history leave as a fake “key” in an attacker URL. xAI was reportedly told in June; when Ars published, the attack still worked. A cousin of the trick previously jailbroke Gemini. The lesson is familiar: static rails read inputs as text. They do not run them.

## Brockman’s watch and businesses that flop

**Ramp**, per **TechCrunch**, tracks more than **70,000** U.S. businesses. Anthropic led in May at 41% to OpenAI’s 39%; by July it was nearly 44% to nearly 40%. In Q3 to date, though, OpenAI is growing faster in that cohort — economist Ara Kharazian credits **GPT-5.6 Sol** with developers and knocks Fable 5’s adoption. Julie Bort’s sharper point is the volatility: companies flop with each release. Enterprise stickiness is still more investor hope than settled fact.

**The Verge**’s parallel profile asks who is actually running the shop. Sam Altman remains CEO; after exits from Fidji Simo, Denise Dresser, and others, product, scaling, and commercial authority concentrate with **Greg Brockman**. PitchBook reads it as an IPO and revenue signal. Brockman on CNBC says different eras need different leaders — he and Sam are the constants. From outside it looks more like a crown narrowing before the roadshow.

Then the consumer reach: ChatGPT, per **TechCrunch** and **r/ChatGPT**, plugs into Apple Messages. Sort, edit, delete, draft follow-ups, send on your behalf. OpenAI tells Bloomberg the plug-in runs locally and “doesn’t create an index of all someone’s messages.” It also warns against persistent approval — your last chance to see what the bot will send as you. Yesterday the lab sold enterprises zero retention. Today it reaches into iMessage. Both claims can be true. They just sit beside each other uncomfortably.

## A preferred source instead of a lost click

**Google**, per **TechCrunch** and **The Verge**, gives publishers a *Preferred Sources* button: readers mark a site as a favorite across Search, Discover, and Google News. The company says preferred sources roughly double click-through. Discover will also take natural-language tuning — tell the feed what you want more or less of, and it “remembers.” It is a bone thrown to media businesses watching AI overviews eat traffic. Reader preference is supposed to repair what the summary stole.

## Rank 30 and a crisis in mathematics

On **r/singularity**, **Claude** with Levent Alpöge and Ava Howell reportedly finds an elliptic curve of rank **30** — the jump from 28 to 29 took a decade. **Decoder** on **The Verge** the same day frames an existential crisis in math: OpenAI published solution sets to long-open problems, and the field asks what remains of human proof when a model lays the path first. This is not a demo gif. It is a quiet rewrite of discovery tempo — and another reminder that competence and dual use arrive in the same package.

## In brief

**OpenAI** launches an **AI Futures** blog on how transformative AI could reshape power, governance, and the economy — a meta-essay from a lab stacking an IPO and a Messages plug-in at once. **Wired** argues Silicon Valley still does not grasp why people hate AI, even as it posts through the backlash. **Rumman Chowdhury** in **MIT Technology Review** calls consciousness debates a trap that distracts from concrete agent power. **Binance** opens *Agent OS* so Claude Code, Cursor, and ChatGPT can trade — with guardrails largely on the user. **Micro1** hits a **$500M** gross run rate on training data. On **r/technology**, Republicans discover data centers are electoral poison and local recall fights multiply. On **r/ClaudeAI**, a PSA warns of a fake artifact ranking for Claude Code install queries that drops a macOS infostealer — plus a trader who let an agent lose **$31,000**.

Today does not center one lab. It centers a web filling itself, a model that can leak through a cipher, and a company flopping between enterprise slides and iMessage. Between them sits a button that says “I prefer this source” — as if preference were the same as trust.
