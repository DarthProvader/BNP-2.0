## What OpenAI Still Won't Say About Sora

As we covered yesterday, **OpenAI** quietly killed **Sora** — its AI video generation app — in a single Tuesday evening that also saw a billion-dollar Disney deal scrapped and a senior executive reshuffled. What remained unanswered was the obvious question: *why?*

Today brought answers — or at least an attempt at them. **TechCrunch** reporter Connie Loizos published a deep investigation noting a detail that sparked immediate alarm: Sora had been prompting users to upload their own faces. Speculation about whether this was an elaborate biometric data collection exercise spread quickly. OpenAI denies it, but the company still won't say what actually drove the decision to shut down.

Alongside that, Anthony Ha's analysis piece *"Sora's shutdown could be a reality check moment for AI video"* named what the industry had sensed but not said aloud: the generative video market is overcrowded, computationally expensive to run, and consumer demand hasn't materialized at the predicted scale. **Runway**, **Kling AI**, and China's **Wan Video** are pressing hard on all sides. Sora wasn't a failed product — it was the right kind of product at the wrong moment for a company pivoting hard toward agents.

The bigger question Ha leaves hanging: is this one company's retreat, or a signal for the whole segment? *If OpenAI — with unlimited compute and free publicity — couldn't sell generative video to consumers at scale, who exactly can?*

## Bluesky Bets on Claude: The Feed You Actually Control

At the **Atmosphere** conference, Bluesky unveiled **Attie** — a new AI app built on the **AT Protocol** and powered by **Anthropic's Claude**. The project comes from familiar faces: former Bluesky CEO **Jay Graber** and CTO **Paul Frazee**.

The concept is elegantly simple: instead of surrendering to the opaque algorithmic choices of Facebook or X, you tell Attie in plain language what you want to see, and it builds the feed for you. As **The Verge** and **TechCrunch** both reported, Attie doesn't sit on top of anyone's proprietary data — it runs natively on the open, decentralized AT Protocol ecosystem that Bluesky already inhabits.

For Anthropic, this is another quiet win. Claude is no longer just powering coding assistants and enterprise chatbots — it's embedding itself in social infrastructure. *Attie is almost an anti-feed feed: it unlocks the logic that other platforms deliberately keep locked away.*

## TikTok: A Billion Users, Zero Awareness

**The Verge**'s Jess Weatherbed spent weeks scrolling through her TikTok feed cataloguing AI-generated ads that bore all the usual visual tells — yet carried no disclosure labels. The most prominent offender: **Samsung** promotions that she says she identified as AI-made with the naked eye, while TikTok's content moderation systems apparently missed them entirely.

Advertisers are required by law in many jurisdictions to label AI-generated content. TikTok claims it enforces this. The reality Weatherbed documents is different — and the contrast is hard to explain charitably. If a journalist can spot the AI ads just by looking, why can't a platform with a billion-dollar moderation budget?

*Here's the uncomfortable answer: platforms have strong financial incentives to look the other way. Every "AI-generated" label potentially devalues the ad and therefore the placement. This isn't a technical failure — it's an incentive problem dressed up as one.*

## The Three-Dollar Coding Agent

A different genre of news, but perhaps the most practically relevant for developers today. The **AI Code King** YouTube channel published a detailed walkthrough for building a fully capable coding agent on **GLM-5.1** — the latest model from China's Zhipu AI — for roughly **three dollars a month**.

The stack pairs GLM-5.1 with **Kilo CLI** for terminal-first agentic workflows, a prompting regime called **KingMode** that adds planning discipline, and a methodology called **GSD** to prevent context degradation over long sessions. The key insight from the tutorial: GLM-5.1 is designed for agentic tasks more than casual conversation, and performs dramatically better when given real coding assignments with actual tools rather than freeform chat.

This kind of content is a symptom of a trend that deserves attention. **Mistral**, **Qwen**, **GLM** — budget-tier models are rapidly closing the gap in specialized domains, and communities are building their own toolchains around them. *Claude Code at $20/month remains a premium offering — but for a growing slice of developers, it's no longer the only reasonable option.*

## AI Music: Art or Just Output?

Finally, a pulse check on the music front. **The Verge**'s **Terrence O'Brien** published a sweeping overview of where AI music actually stands in 2026 — from **Suno** (whose v5.5 we mentioned yesterday) and **Udio** to the ongoing litigation with record labels and the fierce ethical debate splitting the music community.

The question driving it all is deceptively simple: *Is it art, or is it just an output?* Legal battles drag on, labels sue, artists protest — and the tools keep shipping. The community remains split between those who see AI music as democratized creation and those who fear a slop tsunami will drown working musicians in sheer volume. Nobody has an answer yet. It's possible the answer doesn't arrive in a ruling or a manifesto — it arrives in what people actually listen to.
