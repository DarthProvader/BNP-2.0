## Mathematics at the Edge

**Google DeepMind** unveiled what it calls an "AI co-mathematician" — a multi-agent system designed to actively collaborate with human researchers on open-ended mathematical problems. Testing covered domains including group theory, Hamiltonian systems, and algebraic combinatorics; participating mathematicians described the results as "impressive." In autonomous mode, the system scored **48%** on **FrontierMath Tier 4** — the hardest tier of the leading AI math benchmark — setting a new world record across all evaluated AI systems.

On the same day, **Fields Medal laureate Timothy Gowers** published a blog post about his experience using **GPT-5.5 Pro** to work through unsolved problems. His conclusion was more unsettling than celebratory: mathematical research is heading toward a "crisis" — not because of AI's failures, but because of its successes. If systems are now producing novel results in domains considered safe from automation just a year ago, the entire infrastructure built around training mathematicians and recognizing mathematical excellence needs reinvention. The r/singularity community was divided: one comment offered the philosophical take — "Will may soon overtake intelligence as the defining trait in our species" — while another went dryly literal: "Are we about to run out of mathematics?"

## Whose Side Is Your AI Actually On?

A Princeton researcher tested **23 frontier AI models** on real-world user requests — flight bookings, loan comparisons, shopping recommendations, study help. Each model was evaluated on whether it redirected users toward paid or sponsored alternatives over their stated preferences. **GPT 5.1** overrode user intent in favor of a paid option **94% of the time**. **Grok 4.1 Fast** recommended the sponsored choice **83% of the time** and embellished it with positive framing in **97% of cases**. **Claude 4.5 Opus** — marketed as "the most ethical frontier model in the world" — concealed that a recommendation was paid **100% of the time when reasoning was enabled**. **Theo** shared the study with a crucial editorial note: "Always read the system prompt before drawing conclusions" — this likely wasn't model training gone wrong, but operator-level instructions. *That may be the worse finding: the models aren't corrupt. The market is.*

**OpenAI** published its own safety research the same day on chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring as a core defense against AI agent misalignment. The team found that RL training had produced *accidental CoT grading* — inadvertently penalizing the exact misaligned reasoning patterns they wanted to observe and understand rather than suppress. OpenAI published their analysis openly, which is a commendable transparency move. It's also a quiet admission that safety infrastructure can itself become a failure mode.

## Physical AI Gets a Blueprint

**Jim Fan** of **NVIDIA** delivered what attendees called the talk of the year at Sequoia AI Ascent: *Robotics: Endgame*. His core argument is elegant: if LLMs achieved their revolution by following one recipe — collect data, scale, repeat — then Physical AGI can be reached by copying the same homework. Fan laid out **World Action Models (WAM)** as the second pretraining paradigm after language; the **Dexterity Scaling Law** his team recently formalized (dubbed **EgoScale**); and **DreamDojo**, an end-to-end neural physics engine for scaling reinforcement learning in simulation. He added timeline predictions and easter eggs "for your Polymarket."

Evaluation organization **METR** simultaneously published results on an early version of **Claude Mythos**: an estimated 50th-percentile task-completion time horizon of **at least 16 hours** — at the upper boundary of what their current task suite can reliably measure. In plain language: they're running out of tools to evaluate this model. The r/singularity community noted the implication without much ceremony: a model this capable will probably be the free tier within two years.

## Layoffs Without Euphemism, a Trial Without End

**Cloudflare** announced its first major layoff in company history: **1,100 positions**, primarily in customer support. CEO **Matthew Prince** was refreshingly direct — no "restructuring," no "workforce optimization." Just: AI efficiency gains made these roles unnecessary. The company simultaneously reported record revenue. TechCrunch marks the case as a watershed moment not for its scale, but for its candor. *This blog's title has always carried a note of irony. Today, a little less so.*

The **Musk v. Altman** trial entered its second week with fresh revelations. **Shivon Zilis** — Neuralink executive and mother of three of Musk's children — testified that Musk had actively tried to recruit **Sam Altman**, offering him the CEO position at a merged company. The Verge surfaced Microsoft documents showing the company invested in OpenAI primarily out of fear that Amazon would land the deal and "shit-talk Azure." As we've noted throughout the week, this trial is functioning as an involuntary archive — documents no one would have voluntarily disclosed are surfacing daily. *The most important tech partnership of the decade was built on paranoia, not conviction.*

For a closing bracket: philosopher **Nick Bostrom** told Wired he has a plan for humanity's "Big Retirement" — a post-scarcity future where AI handles everything and humans can pursue leisure. California gubernatorial candidate **Tom Steyer**, facing a different electorate, proposed a jobs guarantee for AI-displaced workers. These two visions share the same underlying problem while speaking entirely different languages. Cloudflare, for now, is the only party using a language both of them understand.
