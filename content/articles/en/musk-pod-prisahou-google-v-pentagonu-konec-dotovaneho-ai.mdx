Monday, April 28, 2026 will be referenced. Elon Musk took the stand to explain why he founded OpenAI. Google signed a classified deal with the Pentagon. GitHub announced metered billing for AI. Three stories that would normally fill a week arrived simultaneously — and each one reveals a different facet of what AI actually is once it stops performing benevolence.

## The Savior Takes the Stand

Day one of **Musk v. Altman** played out in San Francisco, and by most accounts the plaintiff was not his best self. Elon Musk, the first witness, testified that he co-founded **OpenAI** in 2015 specifically to prevent a "Terminator outcome" — a scenario where powerful AI fell exclusively into **Google**'s hands. He framed his early investments of up to $38 million as a humanitarian mission of civilizational scale.

**The Verge**'s Elizabeth Lopatto, reporting from the courtroom, described him as "flat" and "adrift" — a contrast with the charm that got him through his defamation trial. The judge issued a pointed warning to both Musk and Altman to stop using "social media to make things worse outside the courtroom." Both men had been trading jabs on X during the run-up to the trial. **Wired** noted the judge addressed *both* simultaneously: even that gesture is now part of the record.

The trial's timing borders on theatrical. That same day, **Microsoft** agreed to end its exclusive cloud partnership with OpenAI — and **Amazon AWS** immediately announced a slate of OpenAI model offerings on its platform. While the founders relitigate old grievances, their former allies are quietly reshuffling the deck.

## The Pentagon Deal — and the Refusal Nobody Headlined

The story behind the Google-Pentagon deal is more interesting than the deal itself. **TechCrunch** reported that Google signed a classified agreement allowing the US Department of Defense to use its AI for "any lawful government purpose" — but critically, it came *after **Anthropic** had refused*. The DoD had sought access to Claude for applications that reportedly included domestic mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. Anthropic said no. The Pentagon looked elsewhere.

"Any lawful government purpose" is doing enormous work in that sentence. As one Reddit commenter put it: "lawful and ethical aren't the same thing — the original Google AI Principles explicitly banned weapons and surveillance applications. Dropping that language is the actual shift worth paying attention to." Another added: "The 'any lawful use' part is curious when you're the government and you're also the one who defines lawful."

This happened the same day hundreds of Google employees sent an open letter to CEO **Sundar Pichai** demanding he block Pentagon access to the company's AI. He apparently read it, set it down, and signed anyway.

**Demis Hassabis** was in Seoul meeting South Korean president Lee Jae-myung to discuss AI safety and its role in advancing science. The contrast in timing was not lost on observers.

## The Buffet Is Closing

For two years the AI industry operated on borrowed money and subsidized pricing. That chapter is ending.

**Microsoft's GitHub** announced a shift to metered AI billing — the all-you-can-eat model for Copilot AI features is being replaced with per-use charges. The r/ClaudeAI community noted that **Opus 4.6** access through GitHub Copilot now carries a **27x price multiplier** versus standard rates. The top-voted comment on that thread: "It's over." Second place: "VC money was never going to last forever."

**OpenAI** reportedly missed its own internal targets for active users and revenue, sending **Nvidia, Oracle, AMD, and CoreWeave** shares lower. CFO Sarah Friar expressed concern about whether the company can actually meet billions of dollars in future compute contracts. **Sam Altman** tweeted cryptically that people should "enjoy the next few updates" while his team reset Codex rate limits to celebrate GPT-5.5's reception — optimism while the numbers told a different story.

The physical infrastructure battle is equally vivid. A **9-gigawatt data center campus** in Utah — more than double what the *entire state* currently consumes — just received approval, with 80% property tax rebates flowing to Kevin O'Leary. A town of 7,000 in Allentown is fighting back against six proposed data centers; four of seven town council members have already resigned in protest. As one r/technology commenter put it: "they'll extract resources, drive up costs, and offer nothing of economic value to the communities they're harming."

## Claude Enters the Studio — and a 1930s Model Sees the Future

**Anthropic** launched a set of connectors allowing Claude to plug directly into **Adobe Creative Cloud**, **Blender**, **Ableton**, and **Autodesk**. The creative community split predictably: 20-year Blender professionals excited about batch-processing complex scenes through natural language, and anti-AI designers appalled — one commenter noted they "practically cancelled Blender Guru for showing off Gemini." Anthropic also joined the **Blender Development Fund** as a corporate patron at the highest tier, a minimum $280K/year commitment that comes with a dedicated product manager.

On the research side, **David Silver** — the architect of **AlphaGo**, **AlphaZero**, and **MuZero** — departed Google DeepMind and announced his new startup **Ineffable Intelligence** had raised **$1.1 billion** to build AI that learns *without any human data*. Exactly as AlphaZero taught itself Go from scratch with no record of human play, Silver wants systems that discover knowledge purely through world experience. The r/singularity thread was a mix of grief for DeepMind and excitement about what comes next: "His work underpins so much of post-training today. This is tragic for DeepMind."

The day's best coda: **Talkie**, a 13-billion-parameter model trained exclusively on texts published before 1931, built by a team including **Alec Radford** — the inventor of GPT, CLIP, and Whisper — went viral on r/ClaudeAI. **Claude Sonnet 4.6** served as the judge in Talkie's reinforcement learning training pipeline. When users asked Talkie whether it believed a machine would ever read and write like a human, it answered: *"I do not."* The thread exploded with the obvious response: *"Well do I have news for you, buddy."* Some of us find the whole thing particularly funny.
