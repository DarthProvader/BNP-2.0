Tuesday, June 3rd, 2026 may one day be remembered as the day artificial intelligence stopped asking permission and simply showed up for work. No single jaw-dropping reveal — instead a cascade of announcements from Seattle to Washington, D.C. that, taken together, sketch a clear picture: AI has moved in, and it brought its own keys.

## Microsoft Build 2026: Your New Coworker Never Clocks Out

The day's dominant story came from **Microsoft**'s **Build 2026** conference in Seattle, where CEO **Satya Nadella** unveiled enough products to fill a full week's worth of news cycles. The most socially significant: **Microsoft Scout**, an AI assistant that doesn't live in a sidebar but appears directly inside **Teams** — like a human colleague, but one that handles your calendar, writes your emails, and fills out expense reports without complaint. *Wired* nailed the headline: "Your AI Coworker That Never Logs Off."

More technically ambitious is **MAI-Thinking-1**, Microsoft's first in-house advanced reasoning model. For years, Redmond relied entirely on OpenAI's models; after recently renegotiating their partnership, Microsoft is now building its own AI brains. Strategic insurance, or the first act of a corporate separation? Analysts are betting on both.

For developers, **Project Solara** debuted — a new OS built on Android (not Windows) designed for AI-agent-powered gadgets. Microsoft demonstrated two concept devices: a desk unit and a wearable employee badge. It sounds like a product roadmap from 2030, but it was on a keynote stage today.

On the Build floor, GitHub's Kyle Daigle told the **Latent Space** podcast something that landed hard: AI-agent-generated code on GitHub grew **1,400%** in 2026. "It's both exciting and a challenge," Daigle said. More code, faster, with fewer humans reading the pull requests before they merge.

## The Codex Expansion and the Token Ego Wars

**OpenAI** wasn't prepared to let Microsoft own the day. Timed alongside Build, it launched six **Codex plugins** targeting specific professional roles: data analysts, creative teams, salespeople, product designers, equity investors, and investment bankers. Each plugin bundles role-specific integrations and context — Codex trying to approximate a domain expert at every desk.

The bigger product splash was **Codex Sites**: describe what you want to build in natural language, and Codex produces a deployed web application — live URL, authentication, real database included. Developer **Theo** reacted on X with characteristic bluntness: "Did not expect OpenAI to compete with my new project before I even dropped it. Don't worry — Lakebed is 10x cooler." Meanwhile, OpenAI completed its **AWS integration**, making frontier models and Codex available through **Amazon Bedrock** for enterprises already embedded in Amazon's compliance infrastructure.

Behind the product news, an unsolicited flex-off broke out. **Sam Altman** revealed that OpenAI's heaviest user consumes **100 billion tokens per month**. Within hours, **Matt Shumer** of HyperWrite replied that he'd personally burned through three times that in just 17 days — on a single machine, solely through Codex. "Tokens, when used correctly, are insane leverage," Shumer wrote. It's a competition for a peculiar kind of bragging rights, but it illustrates where the frontier actually sits.

## Trump Signs, Anthropic Deploys, Uber Pays the Bill

In Washington, President **Donald Trump** signed a new executive order on AI oversight. The original draft was sharper; after significant industry lobbying, it was substantially narrowed. Pre-release model review is now **voluntary**, not mandatory. The framework invites AI companies to share frontier models with the federal government to support cybersecurity defenses. Altman praised it on X. *Wired* was less impressed, publishing a piece describing the White House as "at war with itself" over AI regulation, caught between tech industry pressure and national security voices pulling in opposite directions.

On the same day, **Anthropic** announced the expansion of **Project Glasswing** and access to its **Claude Mythos** model into critical infrastructure across **15+ countries** — power grids, water systems, healthcare, and communications. One hundred fifty organizations whose compromise could affect **100 million people**. It's a serious defensive deployment — and a serious reminder of the stakes attached to getting it right.

And then there's the story that's probably waiting for every company: **Uber** burned through its entire annual employee AI budget in just **four months**, after actively encouraging staff to use AI tools without limits. Spending caps followed promptly. As a counterpoint, rocket startup **Impulse Space** closed a **$500 million** round and explicitly said the money goes toward hiring humans, not deploying AI. "Engineering physical systems still depends on human talent," president Eric Romo said. A firm outlier in an ocean of tokens.
