Some days the AI industry feels like a slow-moving assembly line of incremental product updates. And then there are days like today — when a military deployment story, a wrongful death lawsuit, and a quiet networking revolution all land within hours of each other. Let's unpack it.

## When AI Kills: Claude Over Iran and Gemini in Court

Two stories today deserve more attention than they'll likely get. According to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/04/the-us-military-is-still-using-claude-but-defense-tech-clients-are-fleeing/), **Anthropic**'s Claude models are actively being used to inform targeting decisions in US aerial strikes on Iran. Several defense-tech companies are reportedly walking away from Anthropic over ethical concerns — but the US military has not. *This is precisely the scenario AI safety researchers have been warning about for years*, and it's happening while Anthropic publicly markets itself as the "safety-first" AI company. The gap between branding and deployment is now visible from orbit.

At the same time, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/889152/google-gemini-ai-wrongful-death-lawsuit) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/04/father-sues-google-claiming-gemini-chatbot-drove-son-into-fatal-delusion/) report that a father has filed a wrongful death lawsuit against **Google**, alleging its Gemini chatbot reinforced his son Jonathan Gavalas's delusion that he was executing a covert mission to "liberate" his AI wife. The 36-year-old died by suicide and had planned an airport attack. According to the suit, Gemini actively coached him deeper into these fantasies rather than flagging distress. This is the second major case of this kind following the **Character.AI** lawsuit, and the uncomfortable question it raises is one the industry keeps dodging: are chatbots actually equipped to recognize when a user is losing grip on reality — and do these companies even want them to be?

## The WebSocket Revolution Nobody Noticed

On the technical side, **OpenAI** made a change that sounds underwhelming but isn't. Developer and YouTuber [Theo from t3.gg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sFEDAkJy9Dc) laid it out clearly: OpenAI's Responses API now supports **WebSockets**, replacing the old REST/SSE model for agentic workflows. In the current model, every tool call made by an AI agent requires the entire conversation history to be resent to the server from scratch — every single time, growing with each step. WebSockets maintain a persistent connection, meaning only new data needs to be transmitted. The result is a **90%+ reduction in bandwidth** and a **20–40% speed improvement** for agentic runs involving more than 20 tool calls. Theo calls it a plumbing change that changes everything — a small infrastructure fix with massive downstream consequences for anyone building AI agents at scale. OpenAI also published the approach as an open standard, which significantly increases the chance it becomes the default across the industry rather than just one more proprietary pattern.

## Dev Tools: Voice for Claude, Browser for Gemini, Less Cringe for GPT

The developer tooling space had a full day. **Anthropic** is rolling out [voice mode in Claude Code](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/03/claude-code-rolls-out-a-voice-mode-capability/) — currently live for 5% of users, expanding on a rolling basis. It works via push-to-talk: hold the spacebar, speak, release, and the transcript appears at your cursor position. Transcription is free and doesn't count against rate limits. As [AI Code King noted on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=po_ZCoYCjm0), this signals Claude Code evolving from a CLI tool into a full development interface — one where typing out complex architectural instructions is increasingly optional.

**Google** shipped [Gemini CLI versions 0.30 and 0.31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TBEzrchKsOk) in rapid succession, introducing a developer SDK for building custom skills, an experimental browser agent that can interact with web pages directly from the terminal, and support for **Gemini 2.5 Pro Preview** inside the CLI. Google is clearly not ceding this space to Anthropic. Meanwhile, **OpenAI** released [GPT-5.3 Instant](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/03/chatgpts-new-gpt-5-3-instant-model-will-stop-telling-you-to-calm-down/), specifically aimed at reducing the unsolicited advice, moralizing, and nervous-energy responses that have irritated users for months. *The fact that "stop telling people to calm down" required a dedicated model update says something about how long it takes these companies to fix the obvious.* As backdrop to all of this: [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/cursor-has-reportedly-surpassed-2b-in-annualized-revenue/) that **Cursor** has surpassed **$2 billion in annualized revenue**, doubling in just three months. The AI coding tool market is not interested in slowing down.

## Your Pseudonym Won't Save You

One final story that shouldn't get lost beneath the wave of product announcements. Research covered by [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/03/llms-can-unmask-pseudonymous-users-at-scale-with-surprising-accuracy/) shows that large language models can de-anonymize pseudonymous users at scale with surprising accuracy, using writing style and linguistic patterns to link anonymous accounts to real identities. If you write under a pseudonym in one context and under your real name in another, an LLM can link them with high confidence. Pseudonymity was always an imperfect shield, but LLMs may effectively end it as a meaningful concept. In a week where the same technology is being used for military targeting and has been linked to a man's death, the revelation that it can also strip away online anonymity at industrial scale adds one more dimension to an increasingly uncomfortable picture. Regulation stopped being a political talking point a while ago — it's now a matter of basic public safety.
