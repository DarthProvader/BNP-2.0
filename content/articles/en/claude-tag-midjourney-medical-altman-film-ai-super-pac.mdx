## The Third Paradigm Arrives

**Anthropic** launched **Claude Tag** — a way for teams to work with Claude inside Slack as a persistent team member. This isn't a chatbot bolted onto a sidebar. Claude joins channels, builds up organizational context over time, and responds when tagged like a colleague would. **TechCrunch** framed it accurately: beyond the productivity pitch, this is a data play — Anthropic capturing institutional knowledge and embedding itself in enterprise workflows before anyone else can.

**Andrej Karpathy** offered the most illuminating take. He called Claude Tag the third major redesign of LLM UI/UX. First paradigm: the LLM as a website you visit. Second: the LLM as an app you download. Third — and this is the shift that matters — *a self-contained, persistent, asynchronous entity with org-wide tools and context*, working alongside teams of humans. Karpathy wrote that it takes a while to grasp, but "it works and it is awesome." **Theo**, meanwhile, responded to the announcement by asking Claude when Fable would be back. Developers have priorities.

## Midjourney Wants to Scan You While You Decompress

While the rest of the AI industry argues about benchmarks, **Midjourney** founder **David Holz** is betting on something more physical: your body. **Midjourney Medical** is a new division aimed at reinventing preventive healthcare. Their device — **FUCT** (Fullbody Ultrasonic Computational Tomography) — works by having you step onto a platform that slowly lowers you into a shallow pool of warm water. A ring of half a million microscopic sensors fires ultrasonic waves through your body at a million times per second. The goal: MRI-quality imaging at roughly a hundred times the speed, with no radiation.

The commercial delivery mechanism is **Midjourney Spa** — a 25,000-square-foot facility in San Francisco planned for late 2027, featuring hot tubs, cold plunges, and what the company describes as "cozy rooms of pools of golden light" where the full-body scan happens as a side effect of a relaxing visit. **Fireship** nailed the cultural read: *"The last time a San Francisco company wanted to reimagine my relationship with my body, I ended up getting Narcan behind the Ben & Jerry's at Fisherman's Wharf."*

The skepticism from doctors, however, is substantive. Ultrasound travels through soft tissue but not air or bone — meaning air-filled lungs and the skull-wrapped brain are essentially invisible to this approach. The current prototype takes 20 minutes (the 60-second target is aspirational), and has no FDA clearance. **The Verge** investigated and found the evidence base thin. Still, the larger ambition — a profitable AI company choosing to work on actual human health rather than racing to build AGI — is genuinely distinct. There's a small echo from the Czech side: the **Fantastic Future** community just showed off a vibe-coded tool for analyzing personal MRI data, set to be demoed live in Prague on June 25th.

## Hollywood Went Quiet. A Super PAC Did Not.

**Luca Guadagnino** made **Artificial** — a biographical drama about **Sam Altman** and the founding of OpenAI. Hollywood largely passed. Netflix, **A24**, Focus Features, and Warner Bros.' Clockwork all reportedly declined distribution, according to **The Verge**. Only smaller distributors Neon and Mubi remain interested. Nobody has publicly explained why. But the context explains itself: A24 holds a $75M partnership with Google DeepMind *(as we covered yesterday)*. Amazon is a major OpenAI investor. Studios have built financial dependencies on the AI industry. A film that ambivalently — let alone critically — examined Altman's rise would put those relationships in uncomfortable territory. Call it speculation, but Hollywood's collective silence is too coordinated to be coincidental.

The same structural power reads differently in political form. **The Verge** reported that an AI-backed super PAC spent **$27 million** on a congressional race in New York's 12th district — a local contest that most people outside the area had never heard of. The target: **Alex Bores**, one of the few Democrats actively pushing for strict AI regulation. The industry spent. Bores lost. This isn't standard lobbying. It's direct electoral shaping, done through a mechanism that's legally transparent but politically brazen. If the AI industry is willing to spend $27 million on a single local race, think carefully about what it's prepared to spend on federal ones.

## In Brief: Oracle Cuts, GPT-5 Heals, Google Fires Its Own

**Oracle** laid off **21,000 employees** — routing the savings into AI data center infrastructure, financed by debt, according to **Ars Technica**. The equation is now so familiar it barely registers as news: fewer humans, more GPUs.

On a more hopeful register: immunologist **Derya Unutmaz** used **GPT-5 Pro** to crack a three-year mystery about T cell behavior, as detailed on **OpenAI's blog**. The breakthrough could have meaningful implications for cancer and autoimmune disease research. It's a genuine demonstration of AI as scientific accelerator — no hype required.

And one sharp piece of corporate irony flagged by **Theo**: engineer **Justin Poehnelt** built an unofficial CLI for Google Workspace. It went viral, hit number one on Hacker News, gathered thousands of users within days. Google fired him — two days after announcing an official Workspace CLI at Cloud Next. As Poehnelt wrote publicly: "I think the fear wasn't specific to my CLI; it was a broader fear of what agents meant for Workspace." The AI disruption eating the AI companies from within. That's a different kind of paradigm shift.
