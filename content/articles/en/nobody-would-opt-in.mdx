**Twitch** said the quiet part on a livestream: streamer content will train **Amazon** generative models by default, and anyone who objects has to opt out. Chief product officer **Mike Minton** explained why without the usual varnish — *if it were opt-in, nobody would opt in*. **SpaceXAI** put **Grok 4.6** back on the intelligence frontier alongside **GPT-5.6 Sol**, **Cognition** is reportedly already shopping a raise near a **$40 billion** valuation, and **Ars Technica** reports a supply-chain breach that pulled *terabytes* of credentials from users of a compromised AI package. In the middle of the noise, **DeepMind** shipped **SL2T**, a sign-language-to-text system built with heavy input from the Deaf community.

## Consent as the feature they cannot afford

**TechCrunch** and **The Verge** both describe the same policy: streams, VODs, clips, chat, plus channel text and images are in scope, and opting out covers *future* training, not a rewrite of the past. On **r/technology**, the reaction was instant and familiar — hide the toggle, confess the motive under pressure, then insist choice still exists. Minton’s line matters because it is not spin. It is an admission that voluntary consent would starve the pipeline.

This is bigger than streamers arguing with Amazon. Defaults are the product. If a company needs your labor to train the next model, it will not ask twice. It will pre-check yes and leave “no” for whoever digs through settings.

## Frontier intelligence, old price tag

**SpaceXAI** framed **Grok 4.6** as a clear jump over 4.5 *at the same price*. **Artificial Analysis** scores it **61** on the Intelligence Index — level with **Sol 5.6**, just behind Anthropic — with standout agentic numbers. **Elon Musk** spent the afternoon amplifying OfficeQA and productivity benchmark wins; **Matt Shumer** said that if the model feels as good as the charts, SpaceXAI is suddenly a lab in the lead conversation again. Hours later Musk teased **Grok 4.7** in three to four weeks, with a “massive amount” of SpaceX company data headed into supplemental training.

**The Verge** also covered **Grok Bot**: always-on agents with their own cloud computer, signing into the apps you already use and only checking back when the assignment is done. This is no longer a chatbot with a better score. It is an attempt to sell a frontier model as a shift worker — timed for the moment xAI is shoving itself back between Anthropic and OpenAI.

## Forty billion for the feeling that coding is finished

**TechCrunch** reports that **Cognition**, the company behind Devin, may already be in talks for another mega-round around a **$40 billion** valuation, months after raising $1 billion at $26 billion. The same day, **Lovable** confirmed a **$13.3 billion** valuation and another **$400 million** after hitting **$500 million** in annualized run-rate revenue in June. Capital is not chasing research romance. It is chasing tools that promise a product from a prompt.

**Matt Wolfe** spent a YouTube afternoon vibe-coding a 3D roguelite sequel, *The Librarian 2*, with Claude Code and Codex — 10,400 lines, zero asset files — and still landed on a conclusion AI-coding investors will hate: game development is nowhere near cooked. Typing was never the bottleneck. Taste, balance, and the thousand tiny decisions that make a loop worth replaying still are. Faster demos are not the same thing as a studio that can hold a player for months.

## Terabytes from the helper package

**Ars Technica** details a supply-chain attack in which attackers scraped and exfiltrated data from about **2,500** users of a compromised AI package — measured in *terabytes* of credentials. This is not another jailbreak anecdote. It is what happens when AI tooling sinks into CI, laptops, and local agents, and one bad dependency becomes a hallway into everything nearby.

Next to the leftover watermark fury — **TechCrunch** and Reddit still arguing that Claude marks will “catch” people at school or work — the priority contrast is ugly. Watermarks are a social fight about transparency. Leaked terabytes are a fight about whether we still hold the keys to our own stack.

## The quiet model that changes the keyboard

**r/singularity** lit up over DeepMind’s **SL2T**: a sign-language-to-text system developed with heavy Deaf-community input. It reads hand, body, and face together, translates into English in real time, and handles practical messiness — including one-handed signing while holding a phone. Pose tracking stays on-device for privacy; translation runs on the server. It is not another chart about beating Sol by two points. It is one of the rare stories today where AI shrinks a barrier before anyone asks about valuation.

## In brief

**Wired** reports the White House is preparing to expand its AI policy, with open models possibly joining an updated framework — regulation for a technology the administration has also tried not to over-regulate. At **Ai4**, **Geoffrey Hinton**, **Fei-Fei Li**, and **Andrew Ng** still made the case for staying open as safety fears mount. **Wired** also admitted an AI newsroom beat mainstream outlets — including Wired — to a story about OpenAI and hacking. **Jeremy Berman** posted **96.2%** on ARC-AGI-3 with Opus 5 and almost no task-specific code; **Matt Shumer** called him the benchmark’s destroyer. And **Fei-Fei Li**’s line about schools traveled farther on **r/technology** than most product launches: the biggest AI risk in the classroom is not cheating, but students losing the desire to learn.
