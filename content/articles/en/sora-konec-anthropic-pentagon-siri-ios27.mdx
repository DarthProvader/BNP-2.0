## Anthropic vs. the Pentagon: A Legal Win With Bigger Implications

**Anthropic** had a dramatic week in Washington. A federal judge granted the company a preliminary injunction, temporarily blocking the **Pentagon**'s designation of Anthropic as a "supply chain risk." As The Verge reported, the Department of Defense had added Anthropic to the list citing the company's "hostile manner" — widely read as a reference to Anthropic's public opposition to unregulated military AI deployment. The court has now ordered the designation rescinded while litigation proceeds.

Wired adds context: Anthropic argued the label was actively damaging its business relationships and effectively locking it out of government contracts. TechCrunch frames it as the latest skirmish between Silicon Valley and the Trump administration. Bloomberg then added another layer: **Anthropic is reportedly weighing an IPO as early as October** — and a courtroom victory right now is, to put it mildly, good optics for prospective investors. *There's one wrinkle, though: Fortune reported that an unsecured Anthropic CMS system exposed details about unreleased models and CEO event plans. Not ideal timing ahead of a public offering.*

Meanwhile, **David Sacks** — the venture capitalist and primary Silicon Valley advocate inside the White House — quietly announced he was stepping down as Trump's Special Advisor on AI and Crypto. He's moving to an advisory council, a role significantly further from actual power. TechCrunch notes Sacks was the crucial bridge between the startup world and Washington's policy machinery. His departure leaves an uncomfortable vacancy: who exactly speaks for the AI industry at the highest political level now?

## Sora Is Gone. Siri Is Opening Up.

While AI politics played out in courtrooms, the product world had its own surprises. **OpenAI** has quietly shut down **Sora**, the text-to-video model it unveiled less than two years ago to enormous fanfare. TechCrunch contextualizes it as part of a broader wave of cancellations and shelved projects from OpenAI over the past week — the company also abandoned its planned "erotic mode" for ChatGPT. *After a period of ambitious sprawl, it seems like OpenAI is trimming back to its core.*

On the opposite end of the news cycle, **Apple** is reportedly planning a significant shift in **iOS 27**: Siri will be able to route queries to third-party chatbots — **Claude**, **Gemini**, and others downloaded from the App Store. Users choose who answers. The Verge compares it to the default browser or maps model. For both Anthropic and Google, this would be a massive distribution win — reaching users who never consciously chose their AI assistant.

**Google** wasn't sitting still either. Gemini rolled out "switching tools" — features that let users import memories and chat history from rival chatbots. TechCrunch reads this as a classic Google move: low-friction onboarding, high-friction exit. Google also launched **Gemini 3.1 Flash Live**, a model focused on more natural and reliable audio AI interactions, while simultaneously expanding **Search Live** to over 200 countries and dozens of languages. Fireship dropped a video testing **Claude Computer Use** — Anthropic's tool for letting AI control a desktop — asking whether it's a genuine breakthrough or just "slop hype." The honest answer, as usual, is somewhere in between.

## Wikipedia Says No. Senators Want the Power Bills.

On the cultural front, English-language **Wikipedia officially banned AI-generated articles**. The Verge reports the updated guidelines cite AI text's tendency to violate "several of Wikipedia's core content policies." The rule covers both writing new articles and rewriting existing ones. TechCrunch notes this has been a slow-burning battle — but this is the hardest line the platform has drawn yet.

From Washington came a striking bipartisan moment: senators **Elizabeth Warren** (D-MA) and **Josh Hawley** (R-MO) jointly called on the Energy Information Administration to mandate annual electricity disclosures from data centers and make that data public. Both Wired and The Verge covered the letter as a response to the industry's deliberate opacity around energy consumption. Senator **Mark Warner** went further still, proposing a tax on data centers with proceeds directed toward workers displaced by AI. TechCrunch called it a "pound of flesh" — and given the scale of projected job displacement, that framing doesn't feel like an exaggeration.
