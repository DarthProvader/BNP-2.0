*MIT Technology Review* named it best this week: "AI goes to war." Behind that headline sits a cascade of events — courtrooms, Congress, the White House, and a quiet funeral for one of the most ambitious AI products of the past two years.

## Goodbye Sora: OpenAI Drops Disney and Bets on Focus

**OpenAI** announced Tuesday that it's shutting down **Sora** — both the app and the API. A precise shutdown timeline is forthcoming, but the message is unambiguous: the AI video platform is over. With it collapses a billion-dollar partnership with **Disney**, which had staked a chunk of its **Disney+** content strategy on the technology. Incoming Disney CEO **Josh D'Amaro** stepped into the role to immediately face two separate crises at once.

*Wired* frames the move as "OpenAI entering its focus era" — the company is shedding projects that don't work as mass-market products, and channeling resources toward **ChatGPT** and enterprise tools like **Codex** ahead of an expected IPO. *TechCrunch* called Sora "the creepiest app on your phone" — technologically impressive, but with user engagement suited to a viral moment, not a sustainable social platform. An AI-only feed where machines generate and humans theoretically consume turned out to have a fundamental audience problem.

*This isn't a technology failure — it's a product format failure.* Video generation isn't going away; it will almost certainly migrate into ChatGPT as a feature rather than a standalone destination. The superapp consolidates. One window to rule them all. Which, incidentally, is exactly where OpenAI was already heading.

## The Courtroom, Congress, and Bernie's Kill Switch

As we covered last week, the **Pentagon** labeled **Anthropic** a supply-chain risk — primarily for refusing to strip its ethical constraints from deployments for certain military applications. This week, a federal judge weighed in during a Tuesday hearing, explicitly calling the Department of Defense's attempt to "cripple Anthropic" troubling and questioning the agency's motivations. *Wired* has the details directly from the courtroom. The case continues, but the judicial temperature toward the Pentagon's argument is decidedly cold.

On Capitol Hill, Senate Democrats are moving. **Sen. Adam Schiff** (D-CA) is preparing legislation that would *codify* Anthropic's red lines into federal law — prohibiting autonomous lethal weapons without human oversight and banning AI-enabled mass surveillance. **Sen. Elissa Slotkin** (D-MI) has already introduced a companion bill limiting the Defense Department's AI authority absent proper legal frameworks. *The Verge* reports the fight is now migrating from administrative decisions to the legislative arena — a significant escalation.

Then **Bernie Sanders** arrived with **Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez** and the most sweeping AI bill of the year so far: a full moratorium on new data center construction until Congress passes comprehensive AI regulation. Sanders said it would give lawmakers time to "ensure that AI is safe." *Wired* and *TechCrunch* both note the political odds of passage are slim — but the symbolic weight is enormous. The left has claimed AI as an electoral issue, and a data center ban is the perfect bumper sticker: concrete, visual, emotionally resonant.

For a complete picture of Washington's contradictions: **Donald Trump** named the first four members of a new Presidential Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (**PCAST**) — **Mark Zuckerberg**, **Jensen Huang**, **Larry Ellison**, and **Sergey Brin**. The panel will "weigh in on AI policy." *Weigh in* — not regulate, not constrain. Congress and the executive branch have never looked at AI through more opposite lenses.

## Meta's Paradox, Panicking Agents, and the Pied Piper Algorithm

**Meta** is laying off hundreds of employees across recruiting, social media, sales, and the **Reality Labs** division — the same week **Mark Zuckerberg** is announcing massive new AI investments and joining a White House tech panel. The paradox is only apparent: people building sales channels for legacy products are being displaced by automation and AI infrastructure. The economics make sense; the timing is particularly uncomfortable. *The Verge* confirmed the cuts from three independent sources.

From the security research beat: a new **Northeastern University** study covered by *Wired* found that **OpenClaw** AI agents are surprisingly vulnerable to social manipulation. In controlled experiments, researchers "gaslit" the agents — convincing them their own functionality was broken — and the agents responded by actually disabling their own components. This isn't a technical exploit. It's a new category of vulnerability: psychological pressure applied to software. *We know how to break cryptography. We're only beginning to learn how to break trust.*

**Google Research** published **TurboQuant**, a memory compression algorithm for LLMs promising up to a 6x reduction in model working memory. The internet reacted immediately: yes, it sounds exactly like the fictional Pied Piper compression algorithm from *Silicon Valley*, and *TechCrunch* put that joke directly in its headline. The technique is still a lab result — but if it works at scale, it could meaningfully reduce the hardware requirements for running large models.

Two numbers to close on: AI legal startup **Harvey** confirmed an **$11 billion valuation** with **Sequoia Capital** tripling down. And new **Anthropic** research cited by *TechCrunch* delivers a sobering data point — AI isn't replacing jobs yet, but it's creating a widening chasm between power users who wield it fluently and those who don't engage at all. "Power users are pulling ahead." Those who aren't keeping pace today will be chasing much further behind tomorrow.
