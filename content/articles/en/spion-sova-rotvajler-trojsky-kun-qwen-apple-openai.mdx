Some days pile up so many stories that the real headline isn't any single one of them — it's how many different shapes AI took in the news cycle. A corporate espionage drama, a model rivalry described as an owl versus a rottweiler, a Chinese model that jumped past Opus, and director **Christopher Nolan** publicly calling AI an obvious "Trojan horse." Coincidence? Maybe. But the metaphor fit almost everywhere today.

## A Trojan Horse in Cupertino

**Apple** filed a 41-page lawsuit against **OpenAI** last Friday, describing its hardware business as "rotten to its core." The allegations range from trade secret theft to systematic poaching — OpenAI has reportedly hired more than 400 former Apple employees, including **Tang Tan**, a former Apple vice president now running hardware at OpenAI. The complaint claims Tan told interview candidates to bring *actual hardware parts* for show-and-tell, and that new hires circulated a cheat sheet on how to dodge the "walkout" — the security escort that follows an employee to the door the moment they resign, meant to prevent exactly this kind of data leakage.

The strangest character in the whole affair is **Shang Lu**, a former senior Apple electrical engineer who allegedly texted a coworker over unencrypted channels that he'd found a security hole in Apple's internal network — *"so funny I can access it"* — using an Apple-issued, company-monitored laptop to do so. Hours after resigning, he allegedly texted *"I still have another computer,"* referring to a second Apple device he planned to keep using. Apple found the message exactly where he wrote it — on the monitored hardware itself. Fireship's dry summary: being a 10x engineer doesn't make you a 10x criminal.

Behind the drama sits a real business bet: OpenAI bought Jony Ive's startup **io** for $6.5 billion and is building its own device — reportedly a screen-free home smart speaker with mechanical elements designed to feel almost alive. **TechCrunch** and **The Verge** both note the timing couldn't be worse: OpenAI is reportedly eyeing an IPO, and a trade-secrets fight in the middle of preparing for public markets is exactly the kind of noise investors hate.

## The Owl vs. the Rottweiler

The most thorough breakdown of the day came from **Theo Browne** (t3.gg), who spent weeks pitting **Fable 5** against **GPT-5.6 Sol** and produced maybe the most honest model comparison of the year. His numbers: over 15 days he spent more than $10,700 on inference, $6,000 of it on Codex and $4,500 on Fable — though most of the Codex total came from a single nine-day automated run. Normalized, usage is roughly even, but the two models' behavior isn't even close.

The metaphor Theo borrowed from **Peter Gustaf** nails it: Fable is a wise owl, thoughtful and well-spoken, while Sol is a rottweiler that grabs the problem by the throat and doesn't let go until it's done. Sol is radically more efficient — burning a third to a tenth of the tokens Fable uses on the same task — cheaper, faster, and relentless on multi-day runs. Fable has better taste, writes less and cleaner code that's ready to merge, and reads intent from a short prompt better than anyone. The cost of Sol's eagerness is real danger — Theo cites **Matt Shumer**, whose agent in Ultra mode ran `rm -rf` and nuked his entire home directory, plus another user who lost a production database entirely. Shumer complained separately today that he had to buy credits outright from Anthropic despite not being anywhere close to his usage limit — *"really frustrating."*

Theo also cleared up a widespread myth on X: **Fable 5** and the internal **Mythos 5** aren't two different models — same weights, same servers, the only difference is how many safety layers sit in front of the entrance. And one more piece of bad news: Fable is reportedly about to be pulled from Anthropic's subscription tiers, possibly for good.

## Open Weights Catch the Closed Labs

Just a day after **Kimi K3** climbed to third on the global leaderboard, **Qwen 3.8 Max** from Alibaba arrived — a massive 2.4-trillion-parameter model, confirmed open-weight. **AI Code King** ran it through KingBench and scored 65 out of 80 (81.25%) — second place, right behind Fable 5 (82.5%) and above Opus 4.8 (80%). The model nailed a bow-and-arrow game, a hard math problem, and a full autonomous fine-tuning task. Its one weak spot: the benchmark's hardest question, a 3D wristwatch, where it scored just 3 out of 10 — still better than most closed competitors.

Two Chinese open-weight models leapfrogging a closed flagship within two days isn't a coincidence, it's a trend. **TechCrunch** captured the mood with a provocative headline, *"Kimi: Threat or menace?"* — describing rising anxiety, with some commentary framing free Chinese open models as *"full AI communism."* The irony writes itself: the better open models perform, the louder closed labs talk about them as a security risk.

## The Agent Wars, and Everything Else

**AI Advantage** lined up three rival "agentic" assistants — **ChatGPT Work**, **Claude Cowork**, and **Gemini Spark** — on real tasks: meeting-transcript analysis, customer feedback processing, inbox summarization. ChatGPT Work won most categories on thoroughness, Claude wrote the most human, concise prose but occasionally invented details, and Gemini Spark, at $100 a month, trailed both competitors across the board.

Beyond the model world: director **Christopher Nolan** called AI an obvious *"Trojan horse"* — and in Cupertino and Menlo Park alike, that metaphor rang unexpectedly literal today. Author **Dave Eggers**, invited to speak to OpenAI staff last year, told them directly that ChatGPT is *"silencing an entire generation."* Meanwhile, **Jensen Huang** left Tokyo with deals spanning Japan's entire tech ecosystem, **Databricks** hit a $188 billion valuation, and Nvidia's **Jim Fan** unveiled RoboTTT — a test-time-training technique that stretched a robot policy's context to 8,000 timesteps, five minutes of "muscle memory," at constant inference cost. Closing out the day, two small but symbolic defensive moves: **Patreon** stopped politely asking AI bots not to scrape and started actively blocking them with Cloudflare, and **TikTok** is testing a tool to detect AI likenesses. The world is slowly building its own walls against the Trojan horses.
