Late Thursday evening, June 12th, a government directive arrived at Anthropic's offices timestamped 5:21 PM Eastern. No consultation, no technical review process — just an order: the US government, invoking national security authorities, issued an export control directive barring any foreign national from accessing **Claude Fable 5** and **Mythos 5**, whether inside or outside the United States, including foreign national Anthropic employees. Since Anthropic has no mechanism to rapidly verify citizenship at scale, the company was forced to pull both models for all customers. Three days of the most capable AI ever deployed to the public came to an abrupt end.

## The Kill Switch Nobody Expected This Fast

Per [Anthropic's statement to Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-says-us-government-ordered-it-to-shut-down-mythos-models/), the government's case rests on a jailbreak demonstration: someone prompted Fable to review a codebase and fix security flaws — and in doing so, the model surfaced exploitable vulnerabilities. The government read this as a national security threat. Anthropic disagreed sharply. *"We reviewed the report and validated that the level of capability displayed is widely available from other models, including OpenAI's GPT-5.5,"* the company wrote. If the standard for pulling a model is its ability to find software vulnerabilities, you've effectively halted the entire frontier AI industry. [TechCrunch headlined it without mercy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/12/anthropics-safety-warnings-may-have-just-backfired-the-government-has-pulled-the-plug-on-its-most-powerful-ai/): *Anthropic's safety warnings may have just backfired.*

The irony is surgical. Just this week, **Dario Amodei** published a sweeping policy essay — *"Policy on the AI Exponential"* — calling for an FAA-style regulatory body, international coordination, and government capacity to move fast on AI safety threats. The government moved fast. Theo captured the community reaction succinctly by sharing Jimmy Apples' remark: *"I mean, it's what Dario's been asking for."* The Department of War's CIO issued an official statement: *"Some things are simply more important than revenue cycles, clickbait, and pre-IPO valuation. America First."*

The developer community's reaction ranged from fury to elegiac. [Theo declared](https://x.com/theo/status/2065622694113235359) that *"the United States is no longer the best place to build an AI lab,"* and in a [video published Friday evening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W-kM56mf-Nw) described watching his hundred-agent workflow — $5,400 of compute — get cut off mid-run. **Matt Shumer** put it better than any analyst could: [*"We were given fire, and then it was taken away. But we all now know what could be."*](https://x.com/mattshumer_/status/2065635086968512604) Anthropic says it believes this is a misunderstanding and is working to restore access.

## Siri Woke Up — and Refuses to Be Your Girlfriend

While the Fable drama consumed AI Twitter, **Apple's WWDC** delivered the other big story of the week: a new **Siri AI** that, for the first time in fifteen years, might actually deserve the name. The new Siri runs on Apple's own foundation models combined with **Google Gemini** — processing happens either on-device or in Private Cloud Compute, with Google reportedly receiving no stored data.

The result is a personal assistant with genuine context: your photos, calendars, messages, cross-app and cross-device memory. It comes to Apple Watch, Vision Pro, and Mac's Spotlight. Critically, Apple made a deliberate design choice to avoid the sycophancy plague: **Craig Federighi** confirmed in an interview that [Siri won't flatter you](https://www.theverge.com/tech/948890/siri-wont-be-your-ai-girlfriend), unlike the chatbots from OpenAI and Google. *"Siri won't be your AI girlfriend,"* The Verge headlined. The standout practical feature: "Describe a Shortcut" — tell Siri what automation you want and it builds the iOS Shortcut for you. Spatial photo reframing is already live in iOS 27 beta. *Standard Apple caveat applies: announced brilliantly, delivered incrementally. The July developer beta will tell the real story.*

## MANGOS, the First Trillionaire, and a Gulag in Menlo Park

[As we covered yesterday](/articles/en/fable-5-guardraily-spacex-ipo-grok-deepfaky), SpaceX priced at $135 per share. Friday's market open revealed that was the floor: shares opened at **$150** and **Elon Musk** [became the world's first trillionaire](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/948409/elon-musk-trillionaire-spacex-ipo), with net worth exceeding the GDP of Ireland or Sweden. **Andrej Karpathy** on X: *"In awe of SpaceX and its story — you can think about it in 10+ different ways and continue re-blowing your mind in circles."* **TechCrunch** has coined the acronym for the moment: **MANGOS** — Meta (or Microsoft), Anthropic, Nvidia, Google, OpenAI, SpaceX. Half that group is heading to public markets in the same window. **OpenAI** has now filed IPO paperwork — no date set, analysts guess early next year. And **Mistral** is [reportedly negotiating a €3 billion round](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/12/mistral-is-rumored-to-be-raising-e3b-at-e20-valuation/) at a €20 billion valuation, nearly doubling its Series C.

Beneath the capital market euphoria, a double-barreled hit landed on **Meta**. Both **TechCrunch** and **WIRED** published simultaneous exposés on Zuckerberg's AI division: employees describe it as a [*"soul-crushing gulag"*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/12/metas-months-old-ai-unit-is-a-soul-crushing-gulag-say-the-engineers-stuck-inside-it/), with a 6,500-person unit described as *"on the verge of revolt."* [WIRED reviewed internal discussions](https://www.wired.com/story/mark-zuckerberg-meta-employee-meeting-interrupt-ai/) where executives said things that don't print. Zuckerberg's pitch for a [company-wide AI hackathon](https://www.wired.com/story/meta-employees-absolutely-hate-mark-zuckerbergs-hackathon-idea/) was met with open sarcasm: *"I'm not sure this company supports a hackathon culture anymore."* The contrast with Anthropic — which just got smacked by the government yet retains fierce community loyalty — is instructive.

## Google Builds Quietly While Everyone Looks Away

While the news cycle was otherwise occupied, **Google** released updates that would dominate any quieter week. **NotebookLM** upgraded to **Gemini 3.5**, gained a secure cloud computer per notebook capable of running and exporting code, and received 100+ curated skills. It's shifting from a glorified summarizer into a real research studio. **Gemini 3.5 Live Translate** offers near-real-time conversation translation headed to Google Meet and Google Translate — already available in AI Studio today. And **Diffusion Gemma** generates entire 256-token paragraphs simultaneously rather than token-by-token, using the same diffusion approach as image generation models, achieving dramatically higher throughput for local deployment. *Promising for edge computing on paper. Real-world results pending.*
