## The Negotiator-in-Chief Gets Swapped

**Anthropic** has quietly replaced its CEO at the White House bargaining table. A **Wired** exclusive revealed Wednesday that in high-stakes negotiations over the ongoing **Fable 5** and **Mythos 5** ban, the company has been sending **Tom Brown** — GPT-3 co-author and Anthropic's research lead — instead of co-founder and CEO **Dario Amodei**. According to sources close to the administration, Amodei was considered too much of a *"weirdo,"* and officials stopped taking him seriously. The messenger got replaced.

The ban is now thirteen days old — well past the "24 hours" Anthropic promised for updates in its first statement. Developer and streamer **Theo** (t3.gg) put out a detailed breakdown: no formal executive order was ever issued. The White House gave a verbal directive through the **Department of Commerce**, giving Anthropic **90 minutes** to comply under threat of criminal penalties. The supposed "jailbreak" at the center of the controversy? The ability to do **code review and security vulnerability patching** — exactly what enterprise customers were paying for. Anthropic's public response pointedly noted that **GPT-5.5** does the same thing, a move that appears to have put other AI labs on edge about their own model exposure.

The most dramatic legal development: **Legion**, a US-based AI-native legal tech company, filed a **43-page lawsuit** against the United States of America, the Department of Commerce, Howard Lutnick, and ten additional defendants. The core argument: the government exceeded its authority under the **IEEPA** — a law that explicitly prohibits restricting the export of *"information or informational materials in any format or medium."* A bipartisan group of four Congress members (two Democrats, two Republicans) simultaneously requested the Commerce Department explain its legal reasoning by **tomorrow, June 26th**. On the optimistic side: Trump told an interviewer he no longer considers Anthropic a national security threat, and reports on X indicate **Fable 5 has begun quietly reappearing in Amazon Bedrock Chat**.

## OpenAI's First Custom Silicon: Meet Jalapeño

While Anthropic navigates Washington, **OpenAI** made a quieter but potentially more durable power move: it revealed its first custom AI chip. Built in partnership with **Broadcom**, the processor is called **Jalapeño** — an ASIC (*Application-Specific Integrated Circuit*) designed exclusively for LLM **inference**.

Until now, OpenAI bought **Nvidia** GPUs like the rest of the industry. Google has TPUs; Amazon has Trainium and Inferentia — but OpenAI had no in-house silicon strategy. Jalapeño changes that. *Whoever controls the chip controls the cost structure — and ultimately, what prices they can offer users.* Inference is the operational engine that runs 24/7, representing a fast-growing slice of OpenAI's ballooning infrastructure spend. The chip has reportedly been in development for years, and its announcement landing during the Fable ban saga feels like deliberate confidence signaling about the company's long-term independence.

## Engineers Are Thriving, But Their Token Budgets Aren't

**TechCrunch** published **SignalFire** data that challenges one of AI's most-repeated narratives: *the software engineer is an endangered species.* The numbers say otherwise — engineers represent a **growing share of new hires** and are the most resilient occupational category tracked. Other roles are contracting; the people who build and deploy AI are in rising demand.

The irony arrives fast: in the same news cycle, TechCrunch reports companies are now **rationing AI token usage**. After the "tokenmaxxing" era — when a single enthusiastic employee's Claude habit could burn through an entire department's monthly software budget — firms are installing token budgets, manager approvals for expensive queries, and end-of-month usage reports. *The AI faucet is getting a flow meter.*

## Both Sides Are Scared, Both Keep Running

**Wired**'s Will Knight met with China's leading AI researchers and found their anxieties nearly mirror those on the American side. Both groups talk openly about the risk of a *"Chernobyl moment"* — an uncontrolled race to capability that ends in catastrophe before anyone can respond. *The people most afraid of this race are the ones running it.*

The **Google brain drain** continues: **Jonas Adler** and **Alexander Pritzel**, both from DeepMind, are heading to **Anthropic**, following a long line of departures including Noam Shazeer and John Jumper. Google retains enormous infrastructure advantages and distribution reach, but a sustained exodus from its frontier research teams is a slow-moving strategic problem that higher salaries alone haven't solved.

Quick follow-up from yesterday: the **$27 million AI super PAC race** in New York's 12th congressional district ended. **Alex Bores** — the candidate who ran on AI regulation — narrowly lost the Democratic primary. The Verge called it a draw: the PAC money generated enough public attention that Bores vastly outperformed expectations, making the win far more expensive and uncertain for the pro-AI side than intended. Twenty-seven million dollars on one local primary. Federal AI regulation legislation still does not exist.
