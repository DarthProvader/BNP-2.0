## A New Layer: China

As we've been covering all week, the Fable 5 ban has unfolded in layers — first a jailbreak, then **Andy Jassy**. Today, Semafor adds a new and alarming dimension, as reported by **The Verge**: the White House's decision to impose export controls was driven in part by fears that **Mythos 5** — the internal model underpinning Fable 5 — had been accessed by a group with ties to China. If accurate, this represents a substantially different justification from what was communicated publicly from the start.

The technical irony is hard to miss. According to Mythos's own system card, the Fable series is **five to ten times more robust** against prompt injection than GPT or Gemini models — not by a small margin. An independent cybersecurity firm that reviewed the government's report on the jailbreak described it as a "complete overreaction," noting the vulnerability involved helping patch security flaws — exactly what defenders do, not attackers.

Dario Amodei initially assumed it was a misunderstanding. He requested time. Then he declined to commit to pulling the model. Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessant** personally told him he was making a bad decision. Hours later, Fable was banned. According to Anthropic, the company received **90 minutes** to take the models down — no details on the specific threat, no prior dialogue, no warning.

## The Wellness Retreat That Wasn't — and the PAC That Is

The government's public communication added its own layer of controversy. In briefings to political media, officials suggested they couldn't reach Dario Amodei because he was at a **wellness retreat**. That claim was swiftly debunked: journalist Ashley Vance, reporting from inside Anthropic at the time, stated flatly that Dario was not at any such retreat. *"The feds seemed to be scrambling to try and make an example of Anthropic,"* he wrote. Anthropic rejected the claim as an outright lie.

Beneath the incident runs a political architecture that **AI Explained** laid out in careful detail. In June, Donald Trump announced the US government might take equity stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic, and XAI — but Anthropic was conspicuously absent from those conversations. Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth had written earlier: *"Three months ago, the Department of Defense kicked Anthropic out of our building forever. Every passing day proves why that was the right move."* And Greg Brockman and his wife fund a PAC called **"Leading the Future"** to the tune of **$25 million**, actively backing Trump — a PAC journalists have described as OpenAI-linked. OpenAI formally distanced itself from it; Sam Altman acknowledged the company lobbies "some," but "much less than others." Anthropic does not lobby at all.

White House AI Advisor **David Sacks** suggested the administration's preferred resolution: Anthropic remediates the vulnerability, export controls are lifted, and Fable returns to general release. There's precedent — the US government has a track record of announcing dramatic actions on Friday and rolling them back by Monday morning before markets open.

## Life Without Fable — and Without Slowing Down

While Anthropic fights for its flagship model's survival, **OpenRouter** made a bold claim: their new **Fusion API** allegedly reaches Fable 5 performance at half the price. The mechanism is conceptually interesting — a prompt is dispatched to a panel of models in parallel, a judge model analyzes the outputs, and a calling model writes the final answer. The benchmark OpenRouter cites is Draco Bench from Perplexity, which measures deep research tasks specifically. YouTuber **AI Code King** tested Fusion on practical use cases — 3D simulators, SVG generation, math, coding-style tasks — and found it uniformly disappointing: slower, more expensive, and in several cases worse than a single strong model. *"Anyone can build an agentic contraption customized to their needs and probably outperform Fable if they want. But Fable was great out of the box. This isn't,"* he concluded.

Meanwhile, **OpenAI** quietly strengthened its enterprise position, announcing a **Partner Network** backed by **$150 million** to help global partners accelerate enterprise AI adoption. No jailbreaks, no government ultimatums, no 90-minute deadlines. *Just capital moving.*

TechCrunch also reports that AI companies broadly are preparing for IPOs in what they're calling a "SpaceX IPO wave." The market, as ever, does not wait for political disputes to resolve.
