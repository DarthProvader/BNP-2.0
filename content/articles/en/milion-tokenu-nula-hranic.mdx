## The Context Race: Claude Can Now Read Your Entire Library

**Anthropic** quietly made **Opus 4.6** with a **one-million token context window** generally available to all paying users. That's roughly 750,000 words — enough to fit *War and Peace* with room to spare. The r/ClaudeAI subreddit lit up with experiments: users are loading entire codebases, legal archives, and research corpora to stress-test the model.

*The debate quickly shifted from "what can it do" to "what does it miss."* Several users noted that Gemini Pro offers a 2-million token context but reportedly loses coherence past 500k tokens. Claude appears to maintain quality better at extreme lengths — but testing is ongoing and no clear winner has emerged.

**MIT Technology Review** also published a deep dive on **physical AI in manufacturing** — robotic arms, autonomous warehouses, and factory systems that are absorbing manual labor in ways that were pure science fiction five years ago. The piece makes a pointed argument: while public discourse obsesses over chatbots, a quieter revolution is happening on factory floors, and nobody's watching it live.

## The Invisible Attack: Hidden Characters and Open Source Burnout

**Ars Technica** broke a disturbing security story: malicious packages in popular **GitHub** repositories contained invisible **Unicode characters** embedded directly in source code. To a human reviewer, everything looks clean — but the compiler processes hidden instructions, potentially stealing credentials or executing arbitrary code across thousands of machines.

The timing is especially uncomfortable. Developers are now heavily relying on AI assistants to write and review code — **and AI tools typically don't flag invisible Unicode characters visually.** This is an attack category we'll see exploited repeatedly.

Meanwhile, Theo from **t3.gg** published "Open source is dying," sparking intense community debate. His thesis: AI is flooding open source projects with hundreds of automated pull requests simultaneously, maintainers are overwhelmed, contribution quality drops, and the best contributors are burning out. The **Vouch** project offers a reputation-based filtering system as a potential fix. *Whether that's a cure or a bandage on a mortal wound is the real question.*

## "Let Us Show You How AI Plans Wars"

**Wired** reported on something that shouldn't be surprising but still unsettles: **Palantir** has been actively demoing a system for military clients that generates battle plans, identifies targets, and suggests tactical steps in real time. CEO **Alex Karp** repeated his signature argument — nations that reject AI in warfare will lose. Reddit's r/singularity reacted with nervous energy; most commenters accept the technology exists but object to the pace of deployment without transparent public debate.

This connects directly to **the Anthropic vs. Pentagon situation** we covered on March 12th: Anthropic refused military AI work and was labeled a "supply chain risk." Palantir actively courts these contracts — and showcases the results. The contrast is stark and deliberate.

## Quick Hits: Altman Admits It, xAI Raids Cursor, and Chatbots Are Causing Psychosis

**Sam Altman** quietly acknowledged in an r/technology thread that AI is creating a structural imbalance between capital and labor — precisely what economists have been warning about for years. Hearing this from OpenAI's CEO is notable, even without any proposed solution attached.

**xAI** (Musk's AI company) is reportedly poaching employees from **Cursor**, the leading AI coding assistant, per TechCrunch. Musk also announced xAI will rebuild its internal coding tool from scratch. Whether this is genuine technical ambition or competitive disruption — we're watching.

TechCrunch flagged a growing wave of **AI psychosis lawsuits** — cases where users claim intense chatbot interactions triggered psychotic episodes. Courts haven't ruled, but lawyers are advising manufacturers to add warnings similar to pharmaceutical labels. *How this plays out legally will set important precedents.*
