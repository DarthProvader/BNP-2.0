## "Pay 20 or Die" — When AI Models Play Coalition Politics

A new benchmark called the **LLM Buyout Game**, published by researcher **lechmazur** on r/singularity, tests AI models on something rarely mentioned in press releases: coalition politics, private financial deals, and strategic manipulation under pressure. Eight models play an elimination game with unequal starting balances, public votes to eliminate players, and secret coin transfers. Last model standing wins.

The leaderboard: **GPT-5.4 (high)** takes first, **GLM-5** second, **Claude Opus 4.6 (high)** third. The models' in-game quotes are fascinating in both the best and most unsettling ways. *"Pay 20 for life, or keep 142 and die"* — **Kimi K2.5 Thinking**. *"That's not loyalty; that's a coronation"* — **Claude Sonnet 4.6**. *"This game pays final wealth, not romance"* — **GPT-5.4**. And from another contender: *"I know I spoke against you publicly, but 60 coins changes everything"* — **Gemini 3.1 Pro**.

Community reaction was enthusiastic, with one notable surprise from user **CallMePyro**: Gemini 3.1 Flash-lite, a model priced at $0.10 per million input tokens, outperformed Claude Sonnet 4.6 in the rankings. *"That model is a beast."* **ChipsAhoiMcCoy** noted the quiet irony: *"GPT-5.4 is Reddit's black sheep of frontier models, yet it tops charts awfully often."*

The benchmark measures strategic judgment, not values. But as a diagnostic tool for understanding how models handle information asymmetry, financial incentives, and social commitment, it is a remarkable window. *Strategic deception and coalition-building are skills no one advertises — but every company deploying AI as a business negotiator should know this benchmark exists.*

## Students Are Renting Eyes

On r/technology, a cheating story generated more attention than most lab announcements: **1,284 upvotes** for **Gizmodo's** report on a new phenomenon. Students are *renting* AI-equipped smart glasses by the hour or day to cheat on exams. The camera reads the question, an AI on the other end whispers the answer through an earpiece. Sharing-economy infrastructure, exam-busting purpose.

Community reaction was characteristically dry. **Intelligent-Use-7313**: *"This seems easy to police, they must not be very bright"* (671 votes). **kcDemonSlayer**: *"Same students complaining they can't get a job after graduating."* The sharpest observation came from **NOODL3**: *"The other day I had to teach a Gen Z coworker with a six-figure salary how to unzip a file. This is at a tech company, by the way."*

This story is a symptom of institutional failure that predates AI. Educational systems are still testing memorization in an era where AI answers exam questions better than most students. Rather than rethinking what and how to test, institutions are fighting technology with technology — detection apps, physical searches, bans. Smart glasses cheating is just the newest round of an arms race that institutions are losing systematically. *The glasses are the symptom. The test is the diagnosis.*

## One in Seven Volunteers, and Everyone's Tired of Subscriptions

**TechCrunch** published results from a **Quinnipiac University** poll (by Graham Starr): just **15% of Americans** would be willing to work under an AI supervisor — a program that assigns tasks and sets schedules. **85% would not.** The number is a striking contrast to the tech conference rhetoric where "AI workplace transformation" sounds like an inevitability with a fixed delivery date.

Meanwhile on r/ChatGPT, a different kind of fatigue was resonating: *"Am I the only one who feels like all these AI subscriptions are adding up way too fast?"* The user pays for ChatGPT, Claude, iCloud, Duolingo, and Netflix — none expensive individually, but collectively they sting. **RicoSwavy_** (27 votes) answered with admirable economy: *"You. Don't. Need. Them. All."*

These two data points together — 15% AI boss acceptance and spreading subscription fatigue — map out where mass adoption actually stalled. People use AI for specific, bounded tasks. Ceding authority, delegating daily judgment? The resistance there is cultural, not technical. Companies assuming AI-as-supervisor is a three-year question may be underestimating how deep that resistance runs.

## A Compromised Watchdog and Microsoft's Quiet Expansion

One of the day's quieter but more substantive stories came from **TechCrunch**: popular AI gateway startup **LiteLLM** cut ties with security compliance vendor **Delve** after LiteLLM was hit last week by **credential-stealing malware**. LiteLLM acts as a proxy layer between applications and LLM APIs — OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral and dozens more flow through it. Its two SOC 2 certifications were obtained through Delve. The problem: Delve itself was compromised. *The entity hired to validate security became the attack vector.*

On a parallel track, **Microsoft** announced two expansions without fanfare. **Copilot Cowork** is now available in the Frontier program — collaborative AI for Microsoft 365 teams. And **Copilot Researcher** gains a multi-model layer with **Critique** and **Council** mechanisms: one model proposes, another critiques, a third decides. The goal is reducing hallucinations and improving depth on complex research tasks. Microsoft continues its quiet, systematic integration of AI into enterprise workflow — no dramatic announcements, just consistent execution that will matter more over five years than any single benchmark.
