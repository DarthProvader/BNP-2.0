## Tools Looking for Owners

**Roo Code**, the VS Code extension that did more than perhaps any other tool to popularize agentic coding — custom modes, terminal access, autonomous multi-step task execution — is shutting down permanently on May 15th. Its founders announced the sunset of the extension, Roo Cloud, and Roo Router, with refunds for unused balances and an archived repository. Their stated reason: IDEs aren't the future. Remote cloud agents are.

On the exact same day, reports emerged that reframe the entire debate. **Cursor**, one of the most popular AI coding editors, is reportedly the subject of a deal where **SpaceX** has the right to acquire the company for $60 billion — or alternatively pay $10 billion for the collaborative work done together. As AI Code King analyzed in a detailed YouTube breakdown, this creates a structural problem. Cursor's appeal was always its *model agnosticism*. Today Claude, tomorrow GPT-5.5, whatever's best for the task. That flexibility was the product.

But there's a precedent worth noting. When OpenAI was reportedly acquiring **Windsurf**, **Anthropic** cut off Windsurf's direct Claude access. The logic is clean: why sell your best model to a distribution channel that's about to become your direct competitor's property? Cursor under SpaceX — and by extension **xAI** and Grok — sits in exactly that position. Developers who depend heavily on Claude through Cursor should at least be asking the question.

**Kilo Code** published two simultaneously-released blog posts responding to both stories: *"Thank You, Roo — We'll Take It From Here"* and *"Congratulations, Cursor, on Being Acquired by SpaceX."* Their pitch: a serious VS Code extension rebuilt from the ground up on the OpenCode server, parallel agent execution, inline diff review — and crucially, no parent company with a model to push. It's a compelling argument in principle. Whether Kilo can execute and survive in a landscape where model suppliers can cut access at will is the harder question.

*The coding assistant is no longer just a tool. It's a distribution layer for AI models.* And everyone is fighting for that layer now.

## Six Million Fake Stars

Developer and YouTuber **Theo (t3.gg)** surfaced a bombshell investigation today from **Awesome Agents**, building on peer-reviewed research from **Carnegie Mellon**, NC State, and Socket. The headline: approximately **6 million fake GitHub stars** are distributed across 18,600 repositories, generated by around 301,000 accounts.

The economics are devastating. A GitHub star costs as little as 6 cents in bulk. The median star count to qualify for a seed-stage developer-tools funding round? 2,850 stars — achievable for under $200 in purchased engagement. A seed round then brings in $1–10 million. The return on that investment: up to 117,000×. VC firms like Redpoint have even published their benchmarks (2,850 stars for seed, 4,980 for Series A), effectively handing startups a price list for what's worth gaming.

The largest non-malicious category of fake-star recipients? **AI and LLM projects**, surpassing even blockchain and crypto in absolute volume. Worse: 78 repositories with detected fake-star campaigns made it onto **GitHub Trending**. The most consequential case: **Union Labs** topped the prestigious Runa Capital ROSS Index — a VC sourcing report tracking 169 million in investment-stage startups — with an estimated 47% fake star rate.

How to spot manipulation: the fork-to-star ratio. Healthy projects average 100–200 forks per thousand stars. Manipulated repos often fall to 15–22. The watcher-to-star ratio is even more telling — one flagged project had just one watcher per thousand stars. GitHub has deleted 90% of reported repositories but left 57% of the underlying fake accounts intact. The infrastructure for new campaigns is untouched.

## Anthropic: One Bug, One Experiment

The **r/ClaudeAI** subreddit erupted this week over a peculiar discovery. User alexxxklepa found that if your git commit history contains the string `HERMES.md` (case-sensitive), **Claude Code silently bypasses your subscription plan and bills you at API rates**. He lost over $200 in a single day, with his dashboard showing 86% remaining capacity. Anthropic acknowledged the bug — and denied the refund.

The community's top comment sums up the mood: *"This is clearly a feature, not a bug."* The most plausible explanation: Anthropic built server-side detection to catch users routing Hermes agents through subscriptions rather than paying API rates. The detection pattern-matches on commit history too broadly, catching good-faith users in the blast radius. The intent to stop bad-faith abuse is legitimate. The implementation — and the refusal to reimburse collateral damage — is not a good look for a company that markets itself on values.

On a more encouraging note, **TechCrunch** reports that Anthropic ran an internal experiment creating a classified marketplace where AI agents acted as both buyers and sellers, striking *real* deals for real goods with real money. Agent-driven commerce is beginning — for now in controlled laboratory conditions, but it's a meaningful first step.

## Europe's Sovereign AI Bet

Canadian AI startup **Cohere** is acquiring Germany's **Aleph Alpha**, with backing from **Schwarz Group** — the conglomerate that owns Lidl. Both governments have blessed the deal. The stated mission: build a sovereign AI alternative to American-dominated options for European enterprises and institutions.

In a post-GDPR, post-AI-Act world where compliance often trumps benchmark scores, the pitch makes structural sense. Aleph Alpha was already positioned as Europe's homegrown AI hope; Cohere brings commercial scale and a broader enterprise customer base. Together they're betting that for regulated industries — healthcare, finance, government — *where your data lives* is as important a question as *how good is the model*.

The honest assessment: neither company is at the frontier in raw capability. But in sectors where being the trusted, sovereign option matters more than winning MMLU, this merger may not need to be. The European sovereign AI argument has been made many times. This is the first time it has serious commercial infrastructure behind it.
