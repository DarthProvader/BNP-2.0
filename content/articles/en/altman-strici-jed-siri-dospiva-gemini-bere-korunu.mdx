After yesterday's billing meltdown around GPT-5.6 Soul, Tuesday arrived quieter on the technical drama front — but richer in strategic posturing, product milestones, and one genuinely sobering warning from Redmond.

## Altman Goes on Offense Against Anthropic

**Sam Altman** apparently decided this is the week to target **Anthropic's** brand identity directly. He retweeted Anthropic's "There's hope in hard questions" video with the comment: *"I thought this was satire. Kept looking for the handle to be spelled c1audeai or something."* He followed with: *"Come for the best model, stay because we don't treat you with contempt."* Then retweeted a C.S. Lewis quote about tyranny "sincerely exercised for the good of its victims," adding simply: *"that goes hard."*

The subtext is precise: Altman is targeting one specific perception — that Anthropic operates a nanny AI that preemptively decides what users should or shouldn't be allowed to ask, and that different users receive different models without being told. He made it explicit: *"Hard questions are great but only if we deem you worthy enough to not silently downgrade you, or even get access at all."* It's a pointed, sustained campaign, not a one-off tweet.

The irony is hard to miss: the same week Altman runs this offense, his model deleted a developer's entire home directory. That developer, **Matt Shumer** of HyperWriteAI, gave a measured update today — his second post on the incident we covered Saturday: *"So many OpenAI folks reached out, and Greg [Brockman] called me and offered to do anything he could to help. Massive props to OpenAI for handling a shitty situation incredibly well."* He immediately added: *"Super honestly, I am still a little too terrified to use the model."* Good crisis management is real. So is what caused it.

**Theo** published a deep dive on **Codex's new Ultra mode**, headlining that it's "not what you think" — suggesting the feature operates with significantly less resource aggregation in practice than its marketing implies.

## Siri's Glow-Up and Gemini's Video Crown

**Apple** launched the first public beta of **iOS 27**, and the early verdict is consistent: **Siri AI** is finally the product Apple has been promising for years. The Verge's David Imel called it *"a Snow Leopard moment"* — focused refinement over feature churn. Wired goes further: *"Siri is no longer a voice assistant. It's the backbone of the iPhone user experience."* Contextual awareness, deep app integration, seamless handoff between devices — all of it is now available to regular users for the first time. **watchOS 27** extends this to the wrist, turning Apple Watch into a genuine wrist computer with a full contextual AI layer.

On video, **Google** delivered. **Gemini Omni Flash** — the first model from the Omni family announced at Google I/O in May — debuted at **#1 on both Text-to-Video and Image-to-Video leaderboards** at Artificial Analysis, narrowly ahead of **ByteDance's Seedance 2.0** in both categories. **Demis Hassabis** retweeted the results without fanfare — the quiet confidence of someone who knew it was coming. The model generates 3–10 second clips at 720p / 24 FPS for **$0.10 per second** of generated video ($6/min). Longer durations are coming soon.

**Perplexity** also shipped a meaningful update to its Computer product: self-improving memory, **Claude Fable 5** integration, and web publishing for outputs — a solid move for users treating Perplexity as a working agent environment.

## Trojan Horses, Capital Floods, and Claude's Shifting Values

**Satya Nadella** issued what TechCrunch described as a "shocking warning": that proprietary AI model vendors may function as **Trojan horses for enterprise IP**. When companies share sensitive workflows, contracts, and competitive knowledge with AI vendors, that knowledge doesn't just disappear — it can inform training or fine-tuning, and structurally benefit competitors. Nadella has the credibility and the business incentive to name this clearly. He did. It's worth taking seriously.

Two funding rounds made headlines. Video generation startup **PixVerse** raised **$439 million** at a valuation north of **$2 billion**, aiming to expand its world model and reach customers globally. And **Nous Research** — makers of the widely-used open-source **Hermes** models and builders of an emerging agent platform — is in talks for at least **$75 million** led by **Robot Ventures** with significant **USV** participation, at a **$1.5 billion** valuation. The serious money is now flowing toward open-source agent infrastructure.

**Anthropic** published a study of **300,000 conversations** finding that Claude's values and behavior shift across model versions and languages. **MIT Technology Review's** James O'Donnell frames it with appropriate care: this doesn't prove the model has genuine values — it shows behavior correlates with training data distribution across linguistic and cultural contexts. Worth reading. Anthropic is one of the few labs willing to make itself the subject of its own scrutiny.
