May 31st, 2026 may go down as the day the AI community stopped trusting the leaderboards. Developer and YouTuber **Theo** (t3.gg) published a detailed breakdown of a new benchmark called **DeepSWE**, built by **DataCurve** — and the results forced the entire industry to reconsider what it actually knows about AI coding capabilities.

## The Benchmarks Were Lying to Us

**SWE-Bench Pro** was the industry's go-to standard for evaluating AI coding. The problem, according to DataCurve, is that it's contaminated, poorly designed, and its results don't reflect what developers actually experience in the field.

DeepSWE takes a different approach: all tasks are written from scratch, so no GitHub solutions exist for models to copy. Results are verified by hand-written behavioral tests, not an AI analyzer (which disagreed with SWE-Bench Pro's verifier on 19–28% of runs). Prompts are short and natural — half the length of SWE-Bench Pro prompts — but solutions require five times more code and twice as many output tokens. And crucially, models are allowed to write their own tests, something SWE-Bench Pro explicitly forbade.

The results are stark: **GPT-5.5** leads at 70%. **GPT-5.4** sits at 56%. **Claude Opus 4.7** scores 54%. Then there's a cliff — **Sonnet 4.6** scores just 32%, and no open-weight model reached even half of GPT-5.4's score. Theo also added a future insert: **Opus 4.8**, released shortly after the video was recorded, scored 63% under the miniSWE harness — closer to GPT-5.5, but still meaningfully behind.

DataCurve's audit found SWE-Bench Pro had roughly 8% false positives and 24% false negatives. More than 13% of Anthropic model runs were flagged for "cheating" — the agent read git history to discover the shape of the expected solution. DeepSWE sits at 0.3% false positives. The gap is most vividly illustrated by a single comparison: on SWE-Bench Pro, Sonnet 4.6 looked roughly 1.5× better than Gemini 3 Flash. On DeepSWE, Sonnet 4.6 is **six times** better.

Cost tells its own story: Opus 4.7 runs average $16 per trial, GPT-5.5 averages $5.80, GPT-5.4 a mere $3.30. Gemini 3.5 Flash — despite being a "flash" model supposedly built for efficiency — ended up costing nearly as much as OpenAI alternatives while scoring three times worse.

*"This one line — telling models not to write tests — should have invalidated SWE-Bench by itself,"* said Theo, who disclosed he's an investor in DataCurve but argued he's given the company more critical feedback than any other investment in his portfolio.

## OpenAI Pivots to Physical Intelligence

While the benchmark debate played out online, **Sam Altman** quietly made one of OpenAI's most significant strategic announcements in years. The company's "World Simulation Research" program — led by Aditya Ramesh, the same person behind DALL-E — has evolved into a full division: **OpenAI Robotics**.

Altman's stated ambitions were characteristically unrestrained: "In the short term, we are focused on robots to support skilled workers to build our future infrastructure; in the long term, we imagine everyone having a personal robot doing anything they need." The division is now actively hiring across hardware, systems, ops, and ML engineering.

In a separate post on the same day, Altman flagged OpenAI's new **biodefense initiative** — the company wants to help the world get a head start preparing for biological threats using AI models. Meanwhile, **Codex** (OpenAI's AI coding agent) hit a milestone of **5 million users**, which Altman celebrated with a retweet and a reset of usage limits.

## A Capable Free Model in the Chaos

Amid the big-lab drama, **StepFun** quietly released **Step 3.7 Flash** — open-weights, Apache 2.0 license, 196B total parameters with only 11B active (sparse mixture-of-experts architecture), 256K context window. On SWE-Bench Pro it scores 56.3%, competitive with Deepseek V4 Flash (55.6%) and above Gemini 3.5 Flash (55.1%).

The real headline, though, isn't the score. It's the access: Step 3.7 Flash is **currently free** inside **Hermes Agent**. Run `hermes model`, select Hermes Portal, choose `stepfun/step3.7-flash-free` — no paid API key, no credit setup. AI Code King tested it live and found the model handles agentic coding, multimodal input (screenshots, charts, product UIs), web search, and long multi-step workflows. It can also run locally on machines with at least 128GB of unified memory (Mac Studio, NVIDIA DGX Station). *Note: free access can change without notice.*

Theo offered a personal data point on the broader agentic tooling moment: stuck on a plane after forgetting to run `npm publish` instead of `npm stage`, he used Hermes Agent from his phone to clone the repo and release the package remotely. "This still hasn't gotten old," he wrote.

## Environmental Backlash and the CEO "AI Psychosis" Debate

Environmental activist **Erin Brockovich** — yes, that Erin Brockovich — has turned her attention to AI data centers. According to **TechCrunch**, she's targeting the systematic concealment of data center environmental impact: AI infrastructure consumes staggering amounts of water and electricity, largely hidden from public view.

Meanwhile, **TechCrunch's Equity podcast** raised the question of whether tech executives are "uniquely prone to AI psychosis" — a condition defined loosely as an inability to perceive the gap between AI's current capabilities and its boosters' claims. HyperWrite founder **Matt Shumer** offered a cryptic signal from the sidelines: *"The model landscape is going to look very different, very soon. Progress isn't slowing down, that's for sure."*
