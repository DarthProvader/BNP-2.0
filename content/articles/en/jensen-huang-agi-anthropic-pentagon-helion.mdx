## The Eight Words That Broke the Internet

The biggest story this week isn't a new model release or a mega-funding round — it's eight words from **Nvidia** CEO **Jensen Huang**. On the latest episode of the **Lex Fridman Podcast** (#494, dedicated to the company that has quietly ascended to a roughly four-trillion-dollar market cap), Huang said plainly: *"I think we've achieved AGI."*

As The Verge correctly notes, AGI remains a "vaguely defined term" — which is precisely where the rhetorical power of such a statement lives. If AGI means that today's systems outperform the average human across a broad range of cognitive tasks, Huang may have a point. If it means robust, contextually flexible, world-understanding intelligence — we're probably still far off. Huang knows this. He also knows that every statement like this advances the AI conversation forward, and Nvidia with it. The man who sells the shovels has every incentive to declare the gold rush is real.

## Anthropic: Security Risk or Security Champion?

While Huang basks in the spotlight, **Anthropic** is having a peculiarly surreal week. The **Pentagon** labeled the company a "supply-chain risk" and blocked it from certain government contracts. Senator **Elizabeth Warren** (D-MA) fired back with a letter to Defense Secretary **Pete Hegseth**, calling the move *retaliation* — arguing the DOD could simply have terminated its contract rather than publicly stigmatizing the company.

The timing is almost comedically ironic: **Cisco's** independent security rankings just placed Anthropic models in **eight of the top ten** spots for most secure LLMs, with **Claude Opus 4.5** taking the number one slot. The company the government declared a security risk is simultaneously the company with the best-secured AI models on the market, according to independent evaluation. If this was a political signal, the Pentagon aimed it squarely at its own foot.

## OpenAI's Empire: Fusion, Capital, and Five Gigawatts

**Sam Altman** resigned from the board of **Helion Energy** — the fusion startup he previously backed — precisely as his company **OpenAI** negotiates to purchase **12.5% of Helion's power output**, with an ambition of **5 gigawatts by 2030**, per Axios. The board departure is a routine conflict-of-interest maneuver, but the symbolism is unmistakable: OpenAI needs energy at a scale that conventional grids cannot provide.

Fusion power has famously been "twenty years away" for the past fifty years. Altman is apparently betting this time is different. Meanwhile, Reuters reports that OpenAI is pitching private equity firms a **17.5% return** in exchange for participation in enterprise AI joint ventures. OpenAI is building an industrial empire on multiple fronts simultaneously — and it needs both electricity and fresh capital to do it.

## Claude Gets a Mouse, Sanders Gets a Meme

On the product front, **Anthropic** launched a research preview of **computer use** for **Pro and Max** subscribers — Claude can now autonomously control a computer, clicking, typing, and navigating browsers. It's not entirely new (a limited beta existed before), but broader availability signals Anthropic's confidence in the system's reliability for real-world deployment.

Meanwhile, Senator **Bernie Sanders** attempted a political gotcha moment — trying to get Claude to "reveal" the AI industry's secrets through leading questions. TechCrunch's verdict: the video flopped, but the memes are great. All Sanders managed to demonstrate was how agreeably chatbots can behave — which is *ironically* a real concern, just not the one he intended to raise.

And finally, a story that rattled the developer community: **Cursor** admitted that its much-praised Composer 2.0 coding model is actually a fine-tuned version of **Kimi K2.5** from Chinese startup **Moonshot AI**. Theo Browne of t3.gg captured the mood perfectly with his video titled "Did Cursor really just rebrand Kimi???" TechCrunch added its own dry note: "Building on top of a Chinese model feels particularly fraught right now." The developer community received the news with a mixture of surprise, amusement, and unease.
