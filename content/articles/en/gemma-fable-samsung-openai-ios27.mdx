Two weeks on from the US government pulling **Fable 5** offline, the industry has sorted itself into three responses: wait for its return, migrate to the competition, or — in the most characteristically internet move — name your new project after the forbidden one.

## Forbidden in Name Only

**Gemma 4 12B Agentic Fable 5 Compose** hit Hugging Face's trending list this week, racking up over 6,000 downloads and 200 likes in its first two days. It's a fine-tune of Google's **Gemma 4 12B**, optimized for coding, terminal workflows, tool use, and multi-step agentic tasks — and the "Fable 5 Compose" in the name is an unmistakable nod to Anthropic's banned model. Tribute or provocation? Probably both.

**AICodeKing** ran it through its paces and returned a measured verdict. The headline figure: a claimed 3.5× improvement over base Gemma on Tau 2 bench telecom, jumping from 15% to 55% on a 20-task local evaluation. *Context matters here:* frontier models score above 90% on comparable benchmarks, the test was self-reported, and the evaluation was small-scale. This isn't the next Fable — it's an ambitious experiment with an interesting name.

In practice the model handles single-shot coding tasks well: focused code questions, function refactoring, quick explanations. Where it falls apart is multi-step agentic work — tool call tokens leak, outputs stall or loop, consistency degrades. AICodeKing wouldn't use it as a daily driver for serious agentic coding. For local, private, API-cost-free experimentation under an Apache 2.0 license, available via Ollama or LM Studio, it's worth a weekend.

## Who Profits From the Crackdown?

**TechCrunch** posed the uncomfortable question on its Equity podcast this week: *when the Trump administration goes after Anthropic, who actually benefits?* The answer materialized the same day the question was being recorded.

**Samsung Electronics** announced it's deploying **ChatGPT Enterprise** and **Codex** to tens of thousands of employees worldwide — one of OpenAI's largest enterprise rollouts to date. A year ago, Anthropic was a natural contender for this kind of marquee contract: safety-focused, enterprise-grade, the responsible choice. Today, Anthropic is tangled in regulatory uncertainty, and Samsung made a different call. Enterprise procurement doesn't wait for legal clarity to resolve.

On the misinformation front, **Theo** (t3gg) did a quick cleanup on Twitter: a screenshot from April 2026 showing Anthropic rolling out identity verification via **Persona** — a Peter Thiel-backed third party — for "certain capabilities" was circulating as evidence of new Fable-related surveillance overreach. Theo killed it in one tweet: *"This page is from April and has nothing to do with the Fable ban. Misinformation."* In an ecosystem where anything Anthropic-adjacent ignites instantly, that kind of clear-headed correction pulls real weight.

## The iOS 27 AI Features Apple Barely Mentioned

[Siri got the Wired review yesterday](/articles/en/siri-pratele-whittaker-music-database) — finally a genuinely useful assistant. But **TechCrunch**'s Sarah Perez looked at the rest of **iOS 27** and found the understated story: what's AI doing outside of Siri? Quite a lot. Intelligent notification summaries, on-keyboard text reformulation, AI-powered Shortcuts automation, Image Playground for on-device image generation, and expanded ChatGPT integration for queries Siri prefers not to answer alone. *Most of this didn't make the keynote reel.* Apple is quietly rewriting the text layer of iOS while the conversation stays fixed on Siri's voice.

## Two AIs, One Pipeline

A small but pointed tip from **Theo**: when running **Codex** in an agentic loop, try appending the instruction *"When you are done designing the API, get a second opinion from Opus with 'claude -p'."* He reports it significantly improves the quality of code he gets from OpenAI models. Unsurprising — Opus is sharp at catching logical inconsistencies in API design that pass a surface-level read. But the symbolic shift matters more than the tip itself: multi-model workflows combining competing companies' models are becoming standard engineering practice, not experiments. AI reviewing AI's output. A perfectly ordinary Tuesday in 2026.
