## A Legal Win and the End of an Era

In what amounts to a significant defeat for the Trump administration, a federal judge granted **Anthropic** a preliminary injunction on Thursday night, ordering the Defense Department to rescind its designation of the AI company as a "supply chain risk." *As we reported yesterday*, the judge had already signaled deep skepticism — and the ruling confirmed it. Court records obtained by **The Verge** show the Pentagon partly justified the label based on Anthropic's "hostile manner" — suggesting political motivation rather than genuine security analysis. **TechCrunch** and **Wired** both frame the ruling as a win not just for Anthropic, but for the principle that government agencies cannot weaponize security designations against companies they disagree with politically.

Within the same 24-hour window, **David Sacks** — venture capitalist, Silicon Valley power broker, and Trump's Special Advisor on AI and Crypto — announced he was stepping down from the government employee role that made him the primary architect of America's aggressive AI policy. The Verge reports he's moving to a broader presidential tech advisory panel, alongside names like Zuckerberg and Huang. The man who shaped AI policy from the inside is now just an advisor. *Who fills that vacuum matters enormously:* with major model releases, new benchmarks, and ongoing legal battles on the calendar, America's AI policy position looks suddenly undefined.

## Wikipedia Draws the Line — and Anthropic Steps on a Rake

The English-language **Wikipedia** updated its guidelines last week to prohibit writing or rewriting articles using AI. **The Verge** reports the policy cites AI-generated content's systematic tendency to violate "several of Wikipedia's core content policies" — primarily verifiability and neutrality. This isn't a cultural panic; it's a precise quality-control decision by the world's largest encyclopedia. Reddit's r/wikipedia community greeted the move largely with relief, though moderators noted enforcement will be its own challenge.

The timing carries a quiet irony: **Fortune** reported this week (via FutureTools) that **Anthropic** — the safety-focused AI company that just won a court battle over not being a security risk — operated an unsecured content management system that leaked details about unreleased models and **CEO Dario Amodei's** planned exclusive event. For a company that has staked its brand entirely on responsible AI, and in a week in which that brand was on trial in federal court, it is an embarrassing footnote. Security isn't just about model alignment. It's about the whole organization.

## Apple Opens the Siri Door, Google Raids Competitor Memories

Two large stories from the ecosystem wars. **Mark Gurman** at Bloomberg broke news that **Apple's iOS 27** will let users choose their preferred AI chatbot — including **Google Gemini** or **Anthropic's Claude** — to answer queries routed through Siri. Third-party chatbots downloaded from the App Store will be able to "fetch replies for Siri," The Verge reports. Apple, the company that has historically treated its ecosystem as a walled fortress, is essentially conceding it cannot win the LLM race alone. *That is a bigger strategic admission than the headline suggests.*

**Google**, meanwhile, is playing a different game. It launched "Import Memory" and "Import Chat History" tools in **Gemini** that let users copy their context — personal data, preferences, conversation history — directly from rival chatbots. TechCrunch calls them "switching tools" explicitly designed to attract users away from ChatGPT and Claude. In parallel, Google released **Gemini 3.1 Flash Live** — a new audio model focused on more natural and reliable voice conversations — and expanded **Search Live** globally to more than 200 countries and dozens of languages.

## ARC-AGI-3 and the Models Nobody Has Announced Yet

**ARC-AGI-3** launched this week — a benchmark deliberately engineered to be hard enough that today's frontier models cannot pass it. **AI Explained** on YouTube placed it alongside two other cryptic developments: **The Information** reports that **OpenAI** is preparing a model with the internal codename "**Spud**," with Sam Altman reportedly redistributing team responsibilities around it. More enigmatically, AI Explained cites sources suggesting **Anthropic** has a model in development that the company internally believes will "stir governments to urgency" upon release. What exactly that means is unclear — but in a week when AI landed in courtrooms and legislative chambers simultaneously, it does not sound like marketing copy. **Fireship** on YouTube tested **Claude Computer Use** — Anthropic's newly released computer control feature — and benchmarked it against the competing **OpenClaw**.

A few more items from the day's shorter shelf: **OpenAI** definitively shelved its planned erotic mode for ChatGPT, the latest in a string of side-projects being abandoned in what Wired calls the company's "Focus Era" (following the shutdown of Sora). **Mistral** released Voxtral TTS, an open-source multilingual text-to-speech model aimed at low-latency voice agents. And **ByteDance** brought its **Dreamina Seedance 2.0** video generation model to **CapCut**, with built-in protections against unauthorized use of real faces or intellectual property.
